# Adversarial Verification Report — top 12 common issues

*Companion to `common-issues.md`. Two independent adversarial rounds run 2026-08-06; verdicts folded into the taxonomy the same day. This report documents the method for both rounds, the final verdict table, what changed in the taxonomy as a result, what remains unverified, and what a peer reviewer should still poke at.*

---

## 1. Method

**Scope.** The top 12 issues of the taxonomy (CI-01…CI-12), each run through **two independent adversarial passes**. Round 2 did not treat round 1's conclusions as authoritative — its brief was to re-derive breadth and steelmen directly from the primary sources, cross-checking round 1's prose only after reaching its own count, and to flag where its own verdict *label* diverged from round 1's even when the underlying facts agreed.

**Protocol per issue (both rounds).**

1. **Recount the breadth.** Do not trust the headline "affected frameworks (N)" — recount it directly against the framework-autopsy dossiers (`research/02-frameworks/`) and their primary sources (numbered GitHub issues, CVE/GHSA IDs, vendor primary docs, official postmortems, arXiv IDs), applying a *strict on-mechanism* filter: a framework counts only if its evidence shows the claimed defect mechanism, not merely an adjacent symptom. Rows resting on architectural inference are identified as such, not counted as documented.
2. **Enforce the weak-evidence blacklist.** Verify that none of the corpus-audit-blacklisted sources (SEO-farm quantifications, kdjingpai/scored.tools/sider.ai/aixyz corroboration layers, the $33k/5GB GraphRAG figure, NVD keyword-hit CVE counts, the Pinecone platform-risk claim, etc.) is load-bearing for the issue.
3. **Steelman.** Construct the strongest good-faith counter-argument — defensible-tradeoff, already-solved, inherent-to-the-problem, base-rate, or category-error arguments — and test the claim against it. Where feasible, check "already solved" against live vendor docs, live GitHub issue state, or a live web search rather than corpus text alone.
4. **Would the fix matter?** Test whether the issue's next-gen requirement, if shipped, would be noticed by the documented user base (demand fragments, abandonment postmortems, workaround tools).
5. **Verdict.** `confirmed` (claim survives as written, possibly with honest annotations), `weakened` (core survives but the claim must be restated/narrowed and counts corrected), or `refuted` (move to long tail with an honest note). Each verdict carries a confidence level. Per the taxonomy's own rubric: a claim that survives only after one or more of its clauses are withdrawn is `weakened`, not `confirmed` — even if the underlying facts a later round finds are identical to an earlier round's.

**Round 1** ran as six skeptic batches of two issues each. **Round 2** ran as a second, independent skeptic pass over the same 12 issues, re-recounting against the dossiers rather than against round 1's output, and additionally reading round 1's own prose (and, where already written, common-issues.md's in-place verification lines) to check for label drift — i.e., cases where round 2 reproduced round 1's facts but reached a different verdict *label* because the rubric in step 5 demanded it.

**What round 2 verified live, beyond corpus text (an escalation from round 1's method):**

- Amazon Bedrock's built-in RAG Evaluations (CI-01) — re-fetched live AWS docs, same day the corpus asserted the feature's absence.
- CVE-2025-32711 / EchoLeak (CI-04) — queried NVD's live API directly and confirmed the exact dual-CVSS-score claim (Microsoft 9.3 CRITICAL vs NIST 7.5 HIGH, differing only on scope).
- GitHub issue #5057 (CrewAI, CI-04), #5424 (Pydantic AI, CI-04), #6731 with all 27 comments (LangGraph, CI-12), #4117 full body (MCP reference-memory-server, CI-12), #5245 and #4892 (Mem0, CI-12) — pulled live via `gh issue view`, not read secondhand from dossier summaries.
- LangChain's live `VectorStoreIntegrationTests` source (`libs/standard-tests/langchain_tests/integration_tests/vectorstores.py`, 842 lines, CI-03) — fetched and read in full; confirmed it contains zero filter- or score-related test methods.
- Databricks Vector Search's current documentation (CI-05) — re-fetched live 2026-08-06; the "implement ACLs at the application layer" language is still current.
- A live 2026-08 web search on embedding-model migration tooling (CI-07) — to check the corpus's "nobody ships the fix" negative against the present-day web rather than corpus-plus-model-knowledge alone.
- Flowise's EOL timeline (CI-08, via HN thread 49176920) and the OpenAI Assistants API shutdown (CI-08) — cross-checked live against community/Microsoft Q&A sources, not only the corpus.
- A live web search distinguishing the "retrievalci" bench-v0 preview from the separately-named, more rigorous "EnterpriseRAG-Bench" (arXiv:2605.05253) (CI-10) — surfaced a name-collision risk round 1 had not flagged.

**Constraint noted for honesty (scoped to round 1 unless stated otherwise):** round 1's web-search budget was exhausted in two places. Its CI-01 pass did re-verify Amazon Bedrock's built-in RAG evaluation against live AWS docs (the decisive counter-example), but Azure AI Foundry's evaluator SDK — a second probable CI-01 counter-example — could not be independently re-verified and remains flagged unconfirmed **after both rounds**. Round 1's CI-07 "nobody ships the fix" negative rested on the corpus plus Jan-2026 model knowledge, not a fresh web check; **round 2 closed this specific gap** (see the live-check list above) and the negative held — only third-party blue-green/shadow-index/feature-flagged-cutover DIY guidance was found, no framework or vendor primitive.

## 3. What changed in the taxonomy (cumulative, both rounds)

Applied to `common-issues.md` on 2026-08-06:

- **Verification lines.** Every CI-01…CI-12 entry carries a `**Verification:**` line stating verdict, confidence, and what *both* recounts found (not just round 1's).
- **Steelman lines.** Every CI-01…CI-12 entry carries a `**Steelman.**` paragraph: the best counter-argument at full strength, followed by why it does or does not overturn the claim. Several were sharpened in round 2 with a second, independently-sourced counter (e.g., CI-03's OpenAI "filter hints, not security boundaries" disclaimer; CI-07's actor-mismatch argument on top of round 1's inherency-plus-cheapness argument).
- **Restatements (weakened issues).**
  - *CI-01*: categorical "no framework ships golden-set construction/regression testing in its core tier" withdrawn (Bedrock RAG Evaluations verified live; LlamaIndex's `generate_question_context_pairs` + `RetrieverEvaluator` in OSS core). Restated as "no *integrated, self-maintaining* regression loop in any core tier." The "every other defect is invisible and permanent" flourish withdrawn as hyperbole contradicted by the corpus's own hundreds of visible, documented, sometimes-fixed defects.
  - *CI-02*: "structure-destroying by construction" withdrawn — restated as unenforced convention + missing cross-stage contract validation, the least-contestable core. Five stretched rows reclassified; "dominant quality determinant" softened to "first-order, comparable in measured magnitude to retriever/reranker choice."   - *CI-06*: "broken by construction" and effectively-unimplementable-erasure withdrawn — restated as incremental APIs that *break silently in default production configurations*, plus erasure that is *unverifiable* (no completion proof) rather than impossible. ## 5. What a peer reviewer should still poke at

1. **CI-12's admission-bar exception.** The taxonomy's own bar (§ intro, "no issue is admitted without evidence in ≥3 independent frameworks") is not met by any of CI-12's three legs on round 2's strict recount (≈1/2/2). The taxonomy retains CI-12 anyway, on severity (Mem0's open P1-high data-loss/corruption bugs; NVIDIA's vendor-documented capability regression) and on a cross-cutting negative ("no framework ships an agentic path that is a strict superset of its static path") that survives even though no single leg clears the bar. This is a **defensible, explicitly-flagged exception**, not a silent error — but a reviewer should independently decide whether it is principled (severity can override breadth when the underlying bugs are this severe) or special pleading (the taxonomy is retaining an issue its own admission rule would otherwise exclude). Related: CI-12's rank no longer rests on breadth at all — confirm the paper's prose doesn't cite CI-12's "11 frameworks" or "9 frameworks" anywhere; the only honest numbers are the per-leg ≈1/2/2.
2. **The causal-engine claims.** "Open-core economics evacuates measurement from the core" is an incentive story that fits the data; a reviewer should ask what evidence would distinguish it from "eval is genuinely hard to bootstrap and everyone independently deferred it" (the CI-01 steelman's epistemic-honesty reading).
3. **Remaining superlatives.** "Strongest research-says/frameworks-ignore mismatch," "sharpest ignored-solved-work case," and similar rank claims within the taxonomy are editorial, not measured.
4. **Cross-benchmark magnitude comparisons.** The parsing-vs-chunking-vs-retriever effect sizes (OHR-Bench ~14% F1, Chroma ~9%, BM25→E5 ~+10%) come from non-commensurable benchmarks; the taxonomy now says "comparable first-order magnitude," which is as far as the data goes — any tighter claim needs a single-harness experiment.
5. **Absence claims dated Jan–Aug 2026.** Every "no framework ships X" is a negative over a fast-moving ecosystem. Round 2 re-checked several against the live web (Bedrock, Databricks, LangChain's test source, embedding-migration tooling) and all held — but each should be re-checked again immediately before publication (the CI-01 Bedrock falsification shows exactly how these rot, and it is precisely the kind of claim round 1 got wrong and round 2 caught).
6. **Single-source statistics still present as color.** Mem0's 97.8%-junk audit (CI-26), the 808× re-extraction loop (CI-04), and the 0-for-5 `retrievalci` anecdote (CI-10, now further disambiguated from "EnterpriseRAG-Bench") are labeled as single-anecdote — a reviewer should confirm no downstream text quietly re-inflates them into statistics.
7. **CI-13/CI-14 breadth.** Both counts (12 each) are compiled, not verified by either round; a third skeptic pass is the obvious next step, with special attention to whether CI-13's 40+ CVE/GHSA list survives severity/relevance filtering and whether CI-14's "counter-witness" managed platforms genuinely ship better defaults or just different ones.
8. **The inference tails.** CI-05's ~9-cohort architectural-inference tail and the OSS-platform rows of CI-10 are correctly labeled but easy to misquote as documented counts; check any paper prose that cites them. Also check that CI-05's "7 products / 6 organizations" distinction (Bedrock KB + Kendra = one organization, AWS) is preserved wherever the count is cited downstream — "five vendors" was the exact error round 2 caught, and it is an easy one to reintroduce.
9. **Managed-leader counter-witnesses.** Glean, Amazon Q Business, and Azure Foundry IQ recur in steelmen as "the solved version exists at price" (CI-05, CI-06). A reviewer should ask whether the next-gen framework's pitch survives the rebuttal "buy the managed product" — the taxonomy's answer (self-hosted/air-gapped/regulated deployments are excluded by construction) should be made explicit wherever that pitch appears.
10. **Verdict independence and the two flips.** Round 2 was instructed to re-derive its own counts before reading round 1, then cross-check — a design meant to prevent round 2 from being a rubber stamp. In practice, round 2 reproduced round 1's underlying facts almost everywhere and only flipped two verdict *labels* (CI-04, CI-11), both on the same rubric mechanism (claim survives only with clauses withdrawn → "weakened" not "confirmed"). A reviewer should ask whether two independent passes converging this tightly on facts (while genuinely disagreeing on two labels) is evidence the process is working, or evidence both passes share a blind spot the corpus itself contains. A truly external replication — a reviewer with no exposure to either round's prose — would sample a handful of load-bearing pointers (e.g., Flowise #3570, LangChain4j #2513, CVE-2025-32711, Snowflake's re-embed docs, Bedrock's evaluation docs, LangGraph #6731) and confirm them from scratch.
