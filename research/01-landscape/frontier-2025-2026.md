# The Frontier: mid-2025 → August 2026 — and What Died

**Dimension:** `frontier-2025-2026` · **Date compiled:** 2026-08-05
**Method note:** WebSearch quota was exhausted at session start; all discovery was performed via direct fetches of arXiv search-result pages (equivalent coverage: ~15 distinct arXiv/HN queries), verified arXiv abstract pages, the Hacker News Algolia API, and primary engineering blogs. Every citation below was observed directly in a fetched page or search-result listing this session. Items marked **[listing-only]** were seen in an arXiv search-results listing (title/ID/date/first-author verified) but their abstracts were not individually fetched — treat fine-grained claims about them with one notch less confidence. Items marked **[uncertain]** carry an explicit caveat.

---

## Scope

This survey covers the bleeding edge of retrieval for LLM systems from roughly June 2025 through the first week of August 2026: (1) retrieval interleaved with reasoning and the RL-trained search-agent wave; (2) the late-interaction / multi-vector revival and the single-vector theoretical-limits results that fueled it; (3) just-in-time, test-time, and filesystem-native retrieval (grep-style agentic search, progressive disclosure); (4) cache-augmented generation and KV-cache economics against sparse-attention long-context models; (5) self-evolving agent memory and skill libraries; (6) retrieval-native training; (7) the consolidation of "RAG" into the "context engineering" discipline; (8) enterprise/product movements; and (9) — deliberately — the techniques hyped in 2023–2024 that were quietly abandoned, with evidence of abandonment where it exists. Companion files in `research/01-landscape/` cover the pre-2025 lineage in depth; this file only sketches lineage needed to make the frontier legible.

## Lineage & chronological development (compressed)

A quarter-by-quarter skeleton of the frontier period, from sources observed this session:

**2024 (pre-frontier anchors).**
- MUVERA — Dhulipala et al. — arXiv:2405.19504 — May 2024 — reduces multi-vector (ColBERT-style) retrieval to single-vector MIPS via Fixed Dimensional Encodings; 2–5× fewer candidates, "10% improved recall with 90% lower latency" on BEIR. This is the enabling systems result for the 2025–26 late-interaction revival.
- ColPali — Faysse et al. — arXiv:2407.01449 — Jul 2024 — vision-language late-interaction retrieval over document *images*; spawned the entire visual-RAG ecosystem below.
- CAG — Chan et al. — arXiv:2412.15605 — Dec 2024, short paper at WWW'25 — "Don't Do RAG": preload the whole (bounded) corpus into context and cache the KV state; eliminates retrieval latency and selection error for small knowledge bases.

**Q1 2025 — reasoning models meet retrieval.**
- Search-o1 — Xiaoxi Li et al. — arXiv:2501.05366 — Jan 2025 — agentic RAG wrapped around a large reasoning model: the model retrieves when it detects uncertainty mid-chain, plus a "Reason-in-Documents" module that digests retrieved documents before injection to avoid polluting the reasoning chain. The canonical bridge from pipeline-RAG to reasoning-interleaved retrieval.
- Agentic RAG survey — Aditi Singh et al. — arXiv:2501.09136 — Jan 2025 (revised Apr 2026) — codified "agentic RAG" as a category: autonomous agents embedded in the RAG pipeline.
- NSA (Native Sparse Attention) — Jingyang Yuan et al. — arXiv:2502.11089 — Feb 2025 — hierarchical sparse attention (coarse token compression + fine-grained selection), natively trainable. **[uncertain]** Authors are widely reported as DeepSeek-AI; the arXiv abstract page does not display affiliations, so affiliation is asserted from general knowledge, not this session's fetch.
- Search-R1 — Bowen Jin et al. — arXiv:2503.09516 — Mar 2025 — the reference point for end-to-end RL retrieval: the LLM learns to emit search queries inside its reasoning rollout, trained with outcome rewards and retrieved-token masking for stability; +41% (Qwen2.5-7B) / +20% (3B) over RAG baselines on QA suites.
- MongoDB acquires Voyage AI for $220M — Feb 24, 2025 (Bloomberg; MongoDB investor release; HN 119 pts) — first major signal that standalone embedding/retrieval vendors get absorbed into data platforms.

**Q2 2025 — RL search scales; the "RAG is dead" discourse peaks; code agents drop indexes.**
- ReasonIR-8B — Rulin Shao et al. — arXiv:2504.20595 — Apr 2025 — first retriever trained specifically for reasoning-intensive queries; SOTA on BRIGHT (29.9 nDCG@10, 36.9 with reranker) via synthetic hard queries + hard negatives. The "retrieval-native training" thread begins in earnest.
- Mem0 — Chhikara et al. — arXiv:2504.19413 — Apr 2025 — production-oriented agent memory: extract/consolidate/retrieve salient facts; on LOCOMO claims 26% relative improvement over OpenAI's memory, 91% lower p95 latency and ~90% token savings vs full-context; graph variant adds ~2%.
- ZeroSearch — Hao Sun et al. — arXiv:2505.04588 — May 2025 (revised May 2026) — RL search training *without a real search engine*: a fine-tuned LLM simulates the retrieval corpus with curriculum-degraded document quality; a 14B simulator *exceeds* real-engine training. Removed the API-cost bottleneck from the Search-R1 recipe.
- LlamaIndex: "RAG is dead, long live agentic retrieval" — Sourabh Desai — llamaindex.ai blog — May 29, 2025 — the leading RAG framework itself declares naive chunk-retrieval obsolete: "agentic strategies are table stakes."
- Cline: "Why Cline doesn't index your codebase" — cline.bot blog — May 27, 2025 — a popular coding agent publicly rejects embedding indexes for code: chunking breaks code logic, indexes decay against fast-moving repos, and embeddings are "a secondary representation of proprietary IP"; instead "agentic exploration … No index or embeddings. Just intelligent exploration."

**Q3 2025 — context engineering becomes canon; the theory bomb; obituaries.**
- WebSailor — Kuan Li et al. (Alibaba) — arXiv:2507.02592 — Jul 2025 — post-training for "super-human" web information-seeking: obfuscated high-uncertainty task synthesis + DUPO agentic RL; claims to close the open-source gap to proprietary DeepResearch systems on BrowseComp.
- Chroma, "Context Rot" — technical report — Jul 14, 2025 — 18 models (Claude 4 family, o3/GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5, Qwen3): "performance grows increasingly unreliable as input length grows," even on trivially simple tasks. The empirical kill-shot against "just stuff the 1M window."
- A Survey of Context Engineering for LLMs — arXiv:2507.13334 — Jul 2025 — 1,400+ papers; taxonomizes the field into context retrieval/generation, processing, and management, with RAG demoted to one "system implementation" among memory systems, tool-integrated reasoning, and multi-agent systems. The clearest documentary evidence that RAG consolidated into a sub-topic.
- PyLate — Chaffin et al. — arXiv:2508.03555 — Aug 2025 **[listing-only]** — tooling for training/serving late-interaction models; infrastructure signal of the multi-vector revival.
- LIMIT — Orion Weller, Boratko, Naim, Lee (Google DeepMind) — arXiv:2508.21038 — Aug 2025, **accepted ICLR 2026** — proves "the number of top-k subsets of documents capable of being returned as the result of some query is limited by the dimension of the embedding"; the constraint binds even when free embeddings are optimized directly on test data. The LIMIT benchmark — trivially simple queries — defeats SOTA single-vector embedders. The single most cited theoretical result of the period.
- Latent Space podcast, "RAG Is Dead, Context Engineering Is King" — Jeff Huber (Chroma CEO) — Aug 2025 — "RAG is just retrieval, augmentation, and generation — three concepts put together into one thing"; retrieval systems need "hybrid recall using vector search, lexical search, metadata filtering, and re-ranking," not a standalone vector DB.
- Anthropic, "Effective context engineering for AI agents" — Sep 29, 2025 — canonizes just-in-time retrieval: agents keep "lightweight identifiers (file paths, queries, links)" and load data at runtime via tools, plus compaction and sub-agents with clean context windows returning 1–2K-token digests.
- DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp with DeepSeek Sparse Attention — Sep 29, 2025 (GitHub tech report PDF; TechCrunch: "cuts API costs in half") — sparse attention shipped in a production frontier model, halving long-context inference cost. Directly reshapes the RAG-vs-long-context economics.
- jina-reranker-v3 ("last but not late interaction") — arXiv:2509.25085 — Sep 2025 **[listing-only]**; MetaEmbed (test-time-scalable flexible late interaction) — arXiv:2509.18095 — Sep 2025 **[listing-only]**.
- "Does Generative Retrieval Overcome the Limitations of Dense Retrieval?" — Yingchen Zhang et al. — arXiv:2509.22116 — Sep 2025 — first of the LIMIT-reaction papers.
- Nicolas Bustamante, "The RAG Obituary: Killed by agents, buried by context windows" — Oct 1, 2025 — HN 290 pts / 179 comments. Note: the original URL now returns 404 (verified this session); the HN record and a Jan 2026 follow-up ("Agentic Search vs. Embedding RAG: An Obituary") survive. The most-discussed practitioner obituary of the chunk-embed-rerank stack.
- ACE (Agentic Context Engineering) — Qizheng Zhang et al. — arXiv:2510.04618 — Oct 2025, **accepted ICLR 2026** — contexts as evolving playbooks updated by generation/reflection/curation delta updates; names two now-standard failure modes of naive context self-editing: **brevity bias** and **context collapse**; +10.6% on agent benchmarks, matches production agents on AppWorld with small open models, no labeled supervision.
- Claude Skills / Agent Skills — Anthropic — Oct 16, 2025 — skills as on-disk folders with a `SKILL.md`; Claude "loads only minimal necessary information" per task — progressive disclosure as a product primitive. Followed by "Code execution with MCP" — Nov 4, 2025 — tool definitions discovered on demand from the filesystem; worked example cuts 150K tokens to 2K (98.7%); "intermediate results stay in the execution environment by default."
- LIR: First Workshop on Late Interaction and Multi-Vector Retrieval @ ECIR 2026 — Clavié et al. — arXiv:2511.00444 — Nov 2025 **[listing-only]** — the revival gets its own workshop.

**Q1–Q2 2026 — theory matures; RL search industrializes; memory becomes an attack surface.**
- "ℝ^2k is Theoretically Large Enough for Embedding-based Top-k Retrieval" — Zihao Wang — arXiv:2601.20844 — Jan 2026 — the constructive counterpart to LIMIT: Minimal Embeddable Dimension is Θ(k), independent of corpus size — so the *worst-case* limits are about k, and practical failure is a training/geometry problem as much as a dimension problem. Explicit tension with the popular "embeddings are mathematically doomed" reading of LIMIT.
- LEMUR (learned multi-vector retrieval) — Jääsaari — arXiv:2601.21853 — Jan 2026 **[listing-only]**; Col-Bandit — Roi Pony — arXiv:2602.02827 **[listing-only]**; Multi-Vector Index Compression in Any Modality — arXiv:2602.21202 **[listing-only]** — the MUVERA-opened efficiency race.
- Scaling Laws for Embedding Dimension in IR — Killingback — arXiv:2602.05062 — Feb 2026 — diminishing, task-dependent returns to dimension.
- "Generative Retrieval Overcomes Limitations of Dense Retrieval but Struggles with Identifier Ambiguity" — Bracher — arXiv:2604.05764 — Apr 2026 — GR hits 0.92–0.99 R@2 on LIMIT but collapses to 0.51 under hard negatives.
- Pinecone launches "Nexus: The Knowledge Engine for Agents" — May 2026 (pinecone.io; HN) — the flagship standalone vector DB rebrands around agent knowledge infrastructure, not vector search.
- GrepSeek — Alireza Salemi et al. — arXiv:2605.29307 — May 2026 — trains search agents (Tutor/Planner trajectory synthesis + GRPO) to query the *raw corpus with shell commands*; no embedding index at all; strongest token-F1/EM across seven open-domain QA benchmarks; authors concede pure lexical interaction "struggles with queries requiring substantial surface-form variation" — i.e., best *alongside* semantic retrieval.
- Harness-1 — Pengcheng Jiang — arXiv:2606.02373 — Jun 2026 **[listing-only]** — 20B search agent RL-trained against a *state-externalizing harness*; the harness itself is now a designed, learned-against artifact.
- "Is GraphRAG Needed?" — Long Chen et al. — arXiv:2606.25656 — Jun 2026 — "expanded retrieval does not proportionally improve generation quality"; context-optimized basic RAG cuts 19–53% of tokens and competes with graph pipelines; provides a when-to-use decision framework. The GraphRAG hype-correction paper.
- Safety wave: Safety in Self-Evolving LLM Agent Systems — arXiv:2606.23075 **[listing-only]**; KV-cache side-channel attacks on RAG serving — arXiv:2606.21842 **[listing-only]**.

**Q3 2026 (July–Aug, the last five weeks).**
- "AI Agents Do Not Fail Alone: The Context Fails First" — Fouad Bousetouane — arXiv:2607.14275 — Jul 2026 — position/framework paper: seven context-quality criteria (role clarity, guardrail coverage, instruction consistency, tool-schema quality, grounding sufficiency, injection hardening, token efficiency) as *predictive* indicators of agent reliability independent of the base model.
- "Diagnosing Search Behavior and Failure Modes in Long-Horizon Search Agents" — Qi Liu, Jiaxin Mao, Fengbin Zhu, Tat-Seng Chua — arXiv:2608.01913 — Aug 2026 — six agents on BrowseComp(-Plus): failures split into **retrieval gaps** (evidence never found) vs **utilization gaps** (found but misused); "search effort and answer quality are only weakly aligned"; accuracy tracks *cumulative retrieval recall*, not query count; agents produce "a long tail of low-yield retrieval steps."
- MemArena — Jiadong Zhang, Xiaosong Ma — arXiv:2608.02613 — Aug 2026 — 50-agent / 15-day / 10M-token ego-centric memory benchmark: memory-backend choice beats model-size scaling; "permission-aware access fails universally, with Oracle leaking heavily and other backends too timid to disclose."
- SkillJack — Zonghao Ying et al. — arXiv:2608.03509 — Aug 2026 **[listing-only]** — persistent skill backdoors: "poisoned experiences can be transformed by the agent itself into durable behavioral artifacts." Plus "Benign Alone, Harmful Together" (experience-composition harms, arXiv:2608.01759) and "Authority Collapse at Memory Consolidation Boundary" (source constraints lost during consolidation, arXiv:2608.01679), both Aug 2026 **[listing-only]**.
- "Search, Inspect, Fetch: Exploiting Boolean Retrieval for Deep-Research Agents" — Shuai Wang — arXiv:2608.02751 — Aug 2026 **[listing-only]** — fielded boolean retrieval as a token-cheap agent tool; the 1970s IR stack returns as agent tooling.

## State of the art — mid-2026 snapshot

1. **Retrieval is an agent behavior, not a pipeline stage.** The default frontier architecture is a reasoning model in a tool loop that decides *when* to search, *what* tool to use (web search, boolean/fielded search, grep/shell, vector store, graph), and *when to stop* — trained or at least evaluated end-to-end. The Search-o1 → Search-R1 → ZeroSearch/WebSailor lineage industrialized into a 2026 sub-field of its own: harness design (Harness-1, Harness-G arXiv:2607.27652), turn-level credit assignment (LOTAPO arXiv:2607.13501), abstention-aware rewards (arXiv:2607.10738), plan-first policies (arXiv:2605.28354), verified synthetic long-horizon tasks (SearchArt arXiv:2607.24850, DeepSearch-World's 420K-task environment arXiv:2607.07820). (All 2026 items **[listing-only]** unless noted.)
2. **Representation layer is bifurcating.** Single-vector embeddings are formally bounded (LIMIT, ICLR'26) and empirically brittle even off-distribution-adjacent (agenda-vs-topic failures at 15–21% top-10 precision in scientific RAG, arXiv:2605.07158). Late-interaction/multi-vector — made servable by MUVERA-class encodings and PLAID successors — is the credible semantic-recall upgrade, with its own ECIR 2026 workshop, production toolkits (PyLate), universal multimodal models (jina-embeddings-v4, arXiv:2506.18902), and a compression/efficiency literature. Meanwhile the *cheapest* tools — grep, boolean fields, filesystem listing — were rehabilitated because a reasoning model can iterate on them (GrepSeek; Cline; Anthropic JIT retrieval; arXiv:2608.02751).
3. **"RAG" the term has been absorbed into context engineering.** The 1,400-paper survey (arXiv:2507.13334), Anthropic's engineering canon, ACE, and the practitioner discourse all treat retrieval as one input-curation mechanism among memory, tool results, compaction, and skills. New papers title themselves "context engineering," not "RAG" — including in domains (regulatory compliance, arXiv:2607.24791) that would have been "enterprise RAG" papers in 2024.
4. **Memory is the new retrieval frontier — and the new attack surface.** Self-evolving skill/experience libraries (ACE; Voyager-lineage skill distillation such as COMFYCLAW arXiv:2607.01709; text+code dual memory in Metis arXiv:2606.24151; a dedicated survey of self-evolving coding agents arXiv:2608.03392) deliver real gains without weight updates, but Aug-2026 work shows poisoned experiences persist (SkillJack), benign experiences compose into harmful behavior, and provenance/permissions do not survive consolidation (MemArena; Authority Collapse).
5. **Economics moved.** Sparse attention in production (DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp, −50% API cost) plus provider prompt/KV caching created a genuine alternative regime — cache-augmented generation — for bounded corpora, and spawned a serving-systems literature on safe, position-agnostic KV reuse for RAG (LazyAttention arXiv:2606.04302, HYPIC arXiv:2607.01299, AgentKVShift arXiv:2607.21604, Prefill-as-a-Service arXiv:2604.15039, Grounded Cache Routing arXiv:2605.27494 — all **[listing-only]**). Nobody credible now claims long context *replaces* retrieval (Context Rot forecloses that), but the crossover corpus size below which "cache everything" wins keeps rising.
6. **Evaluation moved from QA to long-horizon research tasks** — BrowseComp lineage, misleading-evidence environments (DRNOISE's "verification inertia," arXiv:2607.17291), citation-trustworthiness audits (arXiv:2607.20872), domain end-to-end benchmarks (FinDeepIndicator arXiv:2608.00764), and memory benchmarks at 10M-token scale (MemArena).

## Thematic deep-dives

### 1. Retrieval interleaved with reasoning; RL search agents at scale

**Foundational trio (verified abstracts):**
- **Search-o1** — Li et al. — arXiv:2501.05366 — 2025 — agentic retrieval triggered by the reasoning model's own uncertainty; Reason-in-Documents digestion module to keep retrieved noise out of the chain. *Limitation:* prompt-orchestrated, not trained; digestion adds latency and its own hallucination surface.
- **Search-R1** — Jin et al. — arXiv:2503.09516 — 2025 — outcome-reward RL over interleaved <think>/<search> rollouts; retrieved-token masking is the key stability trick; +41%/+20% over RAG baselines. *Limitation (from successors):* outcome-only rewards give poor turn-level credit (motivating LOTAPO), and real-engine rollouts are costly and noisy (motivating ZeroSearch).
- **ZeroSearch** — Sun et al. — arXiv:2505.04588 — 2025/26 — LLM-simulated search corpus with curriculum-degraded quality; 14B simulator beats training against a real engine. *Critique:* trains against a model of the web, not the web; distribution-shift risk at deployment is acknowledged in the follow-on literature's move back to verified real-corpus environments (DeepSearch-World).

**The 2026 industrial wave.** In May–August 2026 alone, an arXiv search for RL-trained search/retrieval agents surfaces a dozen-plus distinct papers, each attacking one component of the now-settled recipe (GRPO-family RL + synthetic verified tasks + tool harness). All of the following were observed in search listings (Jul–Aug 2026 unless noted), **[listing-only]**:

- **Training Documents Reranker with Search Rubrics for Deep Research Agent** — Wenhan Liu — arXiv:2608.03527 — Aug 2026 — rubric-based RL rewards for reranking inside a deep-research loop; rerankers become agents.
- **Cross-Domain Hybrid OPD for Generalizable Search Agents** — Hongzhan Chen — arXiv:2608.02101 — Aug 2026 — agentic RL combined with cross-domain expert distillation; targets the known domain-brittleness of Search-R1-style training.
- **CoEvoKG** — Zhaoyang Li — arXiv:2608.01904 — Aug 2026 — search agent and knowledge graph co-evolve: retrieved evidence is verified, deduplicated, and written back to graph nodes; joint training of task generator and RL search policy.
- **Search-GRT: Guided Retrieval Training** — Aounon Kumar — arXiv:2608.00974 — Aug 2026 — RL with guided retrieval for multi-hop QA.
- **Harness-G** — Yanning Hou — arXiv:2607.27652 — Jul 2026 — graph-structured harness giving RL search agents structured, non-myopic credit assignment.
- **SearchArt** — Lang Mei — arXiv:2607.24850 — Jul 2026 — scalable *verified* synthetic long-horizon task generation + multi-stage RL; the data-engine answer to ZeroSearch's sim-to-real risk.
- **From Proprietary to Open-Source: Multi-Agent Protocol Distillation in Agentic Search** — Junlin Liu — arXiv:2607.24280 — Jul 2026 — distills proprietary deep-research behavior into open models, then applies outcome RL.
- **EviBack** — Xiao Ma — arXiv:2607.23955 — Jul 2026 — evidence-constrained teacher backoff for search-agent RL.
- **LOTAPO** — Qiang Zhu — arXiv:2607.13501 — Jul 2026 — leave-one-turn attribution generates *process* rewards from outcome signal; directly attacks multi-turn credit assignment.
- **To Answer or to Abstain** — Fengji Zhang — arXiv:2607.10738 — Jul 2026 — abstention-aware RL: dynamic rewards for declining to answer; search agents finally trained to say "I don't know."
- **Tool-Adaptive LLM Reranker** — Zichuan Liu — arXiv:2607.10555 — Jul 2026 — RL-optimized reranking with tool-calling decisions.
- **GRASP** — Varun Gandhi — arXiv:2607.10463 — Jul 2026 — granularity-aware policy coordinating multiple retrieval tools of different resolutions.
- **Harness-1** — Pengcheng Jiang — arXiv:2606.02373 — Jun 2026 — a 20B search agent RL-trained against a *state-externalizing* harness: agent state lives in the environment, not the transcript.
- **Plan Before Search** — Zhipeng Qian — arXiv:2605.28354 — May 2026 — self-bootstrapping RL that forces explicit plans before multi-hop search.

The trend across these fourteen: nobody is arguing about *whether* to RL-train retrieval anymore; the contested surface is reward shaping, credit assignment, abstention, and — notably — the *harness* (the state/tool environment) as a designed artifact that is co-optimized with the policy. This is a fundamentally different research object than a 2024 "RAG pipeline."

**What the diagnostics say (verified):** arXiv:2608.01913 shows top agents win by issuing *fewer, better* queries — cumulative recall, not effort, predicts accuracy — and that current agents lack evidence-sufficiency stopping criteria; they recommend "stopping criteria based on evidence sufficiency rather than search count." This is the single most actionable failure result of the period for framework design.

### 2. Late-interaction & multi-vector revival; single-vector limits

- **LIMIT** (arXiv:2508.21038, ICLR'26, verified) supplies the theory: representable top-k result sets are bounded by embedding dimension (a sign-rank-style argument); free-embedding optimization cannot escape it; simple natural-language instantiations break SOTA embedders. Its own framing: "the single vector embedding paradigm itself requires rethinking."
- **The pushback** (verified): arXiv:2601.20844 proves MED = Θ(k) independent of corpus size m — i.e., for realistic k, a few-thousand-dimension embedding is *worst-case sufficient*; the practical failures are therefore geometry/training artifacts, not pure impossibility. arXiv:2602.05062 adds empirical scaling laws with diminishing returns. **Disagreement to preserve in the paper:** LIMIT is routinely cited (including by practitioners) as "embeddings can't do retrieval"; the 2026 theory says the truth is narrower — *fixed pre-computed single-vector similarity cannot represent arbitrary combinatorial relevance*, which is an argument for multi-vector, generative, or interaction-time scoring, not for abandoning dense retrieval.
- **Escape routes measured on LIMIT:** generative retrieval nearly solves it (0.92–0.99 R@2) but fails under identifier ambiguity/hard negatives (0.51 R@2) (arXiv:2604.05764, verified). Late interaction is the other route, and it matured operationally: MUVERA FDEs (verified) made ColBERT-style scoring ANN-compatible; PLAID-line optimizations continue (PLAID-PRF arXiv:2607.18626 **[listing-only]**); compression is an active race (MarginMerge arXiv:2608.02969; training-free compression comparison by Jha arXiv:2603.22434; learn-to-pool arXiv:2607.06036; any-modality index compression arXiv:2602.21202 — all **[listing-only]**).
- **ColPali ecosystem:** visual late interaction is now default for document AI — ColGraphRAG (multimodal GraphRAG evidence retrieval, arXiv:2607.16208), Argus-Retriever (region-aware query-conditioned MoE, arXiv:2606.04300), enterprise-scale AMES (arXiv:2603.13537), Visual RAG Toolkit (arXiv:2602.12510) — all **[listing-only]** — plus jina-embeddings-v4 unifying single- and multi-vector multimodal output (arXiv:2506.18902 **[listing-only]**).
- **New failure surface:** "Spike Hijacking in Late-Interaction Retrieval" (arXiv:2604.05253 **[listing-only]**) — adversarial token-level score spikes; multi-vector scoring has its own poisoning modes.

### 3. Just-in-time / test-time retrieval: the filesystem is the index

The most striking architectural shift of the period: frontier agent products largely stopped pre-indexing.
- **Anthropic canon (verified):** agents hold *references* (paths, queries, links) and pull content at runtime; Claude Skills load `SKILL.md` folders on demand; MCP tools are discovered progressively from the filesystem and invoked via code, keeping intermediate data out of context (150K→2K tokens in the worked example).
- **Cline (verified, May 2025):** explicit anti-index position — chunking fragments code, indexes decay, embeddings are an IP liability; replace with ripgrep-style agentic exploration. Corroborated academically by "Deep Agentic Search for Repository-Level Code QA" (arXiv:2608.01507 **[listing-only]**).
- **GrepSeek (verified, May 2026)** closes the loop: *train* the agent with RL to operate shell commands over the raw corpus, beating embedding-based baselines on 7 QA benchmarks — while explicitly conceding the lexical ceiling (surface-form variation). "Search, Inspect, Fetch" (arXiv:2608.02751) adds fielded boolean retrieval for token economy.
- **Synthesis:** the emerging consensus stack is *hybrid by tool choice rather than by score fusion* — the agent picks grep/boolean for precision-shaped queries and (multi-vector) semantic search for recall-shaped ones. No observed 2026 source proposes going back to a single fused retriever; the fusion point moved from the ranker into the policy.

### 4. Cache-augmented generation vs RAG economics

- CAG (arXiv:2412.15605, WWW'25, verified) staked the claim for bounded corpora. Context Rot (Chroma, verified) bounds the claim: quality degrades non-uniformly with length on 18 models, so "preload everything" buys latency/simplicity at accuracy risk beyond modest sizes.
- Production sparse attention changed the constants: DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp/DSA halved long-context API cost (TechCrunch + tech-report PDF, Sep 2025, verified via HN records); NSA (arXiv:2502.11089) is the trainable-sparsity research anchor.
- A dense 2026 serving literature now treats **retrieved-chunk KV caches as first-class objects**: position-agnostic/deferred positional encoding for cache reuse across contexts (LazyAttention, HYPIC, SparseX arXiv:2606.01751), query-aware cache fusion (QCFuse arXiv:2606.05875), prefetching (PCR arXiv:2603.23049), cross-datacenter prefill markets (arXiv:2604.15039), agent-memory-specific reuse (AgentKVShift arXiv:2607.21604), and — critically — *correctness* conditions for reuse ("Grounded Cache Routing: When Is It Safe to Reuse an Answer?" arXiv:2605.27494) and *security*: KV caches leak (side-channel attacks on non-prefix KV cache in RAG, arXiv:2606.21842). All **[listing-only]**.
- **Net position (my synthesis):** the RAG-vs-long-context debate resolved into an engineering frontier — a cost/freshness/accuracy Pareto surface where the framework decision is *which KV state to materialize, cache, and reuse*, not whether to retrieve.

### 5. Self-evolving memory & skill libraries (Voyager lineage)

- ACE (verified) is the flagship: additive, curated delta updates to an evolving playbook, explicitly designed against **brevity bias** (summarization erases domain detail) and **context collapse** (iterative rewriting degenerates the store). No labels; execution feedback only.
- The Voyager pattern (skills as reusable code) generalized far beyond Minecraft. From Jun–Aug 2026 listings (**[listing-only]**): COMFYCLAW "distills trajectories into reusable Agent Skills" for image-generation workflows (arXiv:2607.01709); Metis bridges text memory and callable tools in a hierarchical dual representation (arXiv:2606.24151); UniMem couples episodic and parametric memory to balance plasticity and stability (arXiv:2607.26017); CrystalMem targets "memory hysteresis" with crystallization-energy scheduling (arXiv:2608.00303); Self-Play Meets Skill Evolution co-evolves task generation and skill memory for search agents (arXiv:2607.29468); "Learning on the Job" does continual learning from deployment feedback for frozen-weights agents (arXiv:2607.22157); KnowAct-GUIClaw ships an experience-attributable, self-evolving skill library for GUI agents (arXiv:2607.12625); SymbOmni names the problem all of these attack — the "perpetual novice" (arXiv:2607.12042). A dedicated survey now exists (Self-Evolving Coding Agents, arXiv:2608.03392: agents improving via "updating framework, memory, skills, tools"). Claude Skills (Oct 2025) is the same pattern as a shipped product primitive, and this session's own runtime exposes a skill-loading tool — the pattern is deployed, not speculative.
- **Benchmarks & failures (verified):** MemArena — backend choice > model scale; permission-aware access "fails universally." Mem0 (verified) remains the production-memory reference with honest cost/latency accounting, though LOCOMO-based evaluation is contested territory in the broader literature (see companion memory file).
- **Security consensus forming (Aug 2026):** self-evolution is a persistence mechanism for attacks — SkillJack backdoors, experience-composition harms, authority collapse at consolidation, module-lifecycle attack surface (arXiv:2606.23075). Any next-gen framework that writes to its own memory must treat writes as a trust boundary.

### 6. Retrieval-native model training

- ReasonIR (verified) — train the *retriever* for reasoning queries. Rubric-RL rerankers (arXiv:2608.03527) and tool-adaptive LLM rerankers (arXiv:2607.10555) — train the *reranker* as an agent. "Deep Research Pretraining via Predictive Navigation" (arXiv:2608.00432 **[listing-only]**) — push navigation supervision into *pretraining*. Together with Search-R1-line RL, the direction is unmistakable: retrieval competence is becoming a *model capability shaped in training*, shrinking the role of orchestration-layer cleverness — a direct strategic threat to pipeline-style RAG frameworks.

### 6b. Deep research systems: the benchmark and reliability wave

"Deep research" (OpenAI's Feb-2025 product term, generalized) became the flagship application for agentic retrieval, and 2026 produced a dense evaluation-and-reliability literature around it. Observed in listings (Jul–Aug 2026, **[listing-only]** unless noted):

- **Benchmarks moved to end-to-end, domain-grounded tasks:** FinDeepIndicator (four-stage financial-indicator construction, arXiv:2608.00764); FinanceComplexQA (2,026 deep-research tasks over complex financial layouts, arXiv:2607.19238); FinResearchBench II (consensus-derived gold rubrics without expert execution, arXiv:2607.12252); WorldCupArena (dynamic temporal forecasting benchmark designed to prevent answer leakage, arXiv:2607.18084); MMU-RAGent @ NeurIPS 2025 (competition-driven multi-agent RAG systems, arXiv:2606.11199).
- **Reliability under adversarial evidence:** DRNOISE (arXiv:2607.17291) names **verification inertia** — agents stop verifying once a narrative forms; "Is Deep Research Reliable? Misleading Knowledge Induces False Conclusions" (arXiv:2607.20891) builds MisKnow-Agent to measure false-conclusion adoption; LegalCiteTrust (arXiv:2607.20872) audits citations on Existence/Fidelity/Applicability axes; SciHazard (arXiv:2607.18665) finds deep-research agents score 32.3% *higher* on decomposed harm than plain LLMs — capability amplifies misuse.
- **Architecture experiments:** WebSwarm (recursive multi-agent delegation for deep-and-wide search, arXiv:2607.08662); Baikal (structured search over data lakes via semantic-region clustering, arXiv:2607.27562); AREX (inner research loop + outer self-improvement loop, arXiv:2607.21461); on-device deep research at 4B parameters with an explicit "exposure bounds faithfulness, retrieval bounds coverage" tradeoff (arXiv:2607.12257); DeepSearch-World (420K verifiable multi-hop tasks as a self-distillation environment, arXiv:2607.07820); "Deep Research Pretraining via Predictive Navigation" (navigation supervision mined from natural evidence structures at *pretraining* time, arXiv:2608.00432).

Synthesis: the deep-research application forced the field to confront exactly the failure axes a next-generation retrieval framework must own — evidence sufficiency, verification pressure, citation fidelity, and cost budgets — because report-length outputs make retrieval errors legible in a way single-answer QA never did.

### 9. Credible positions & predictions (with disagreements)

- **Jeff Huber (Chroma, Aug 2025):** kill the term, keep the substance; retrieval = hybrid recall (vector + lexical + metadata + rerank) inside context engineering, identical for chat and agents. *Interest note:* Chroma sells retrieval infrastructure; "context engineering" enlarges its market beyond "vector DB."
- **Anthropic (Sep–Nov 2025):** the future default is *agentic just-in-time* retrieval over lightweight references, progressive disclosure, and code-mediated tool access; embedding indexes are one tool, not the substrate. Backed by shipped products (Skills, MCP code execution).
- **Nicolas Bustamante (Oct 2025 → Jan 2026):** maximalist position — embedding RAG loses to agentic search outright; he doubled down in Jan 2026 ("Agentic Search vs. Embedding RAG: An Obituary"). Highest community engagement; weakest hedging.
- **Hamel Husain (Jul 2025):** deflationary position — "RAG" was always just retrieval + prompting; the death discourse is a category error; evaluation and retrieval quality remain the real work.
- **Fouad Bousetouane (Jul 2026, two papers):** the research-flavored synthesis — context quality is measurable, predictive of agent reliability, and should gate production deployment ("capability is not production readiness").
- **Where they disagree:** whether *embeddings* remain load-bearing (Bustamante: no; Huber/Anthropic: yes, as one recall channel; GrepSeek empirically: lexical alone hits a ceiling), and whether the change is architectural (agents) or merely terminological (Hamel). The 2026 arXiv record sides with "architectural": the RL-search-agent and harness-design literatures have no 2024-pipeline analogue.
- **Consensus prediction implicit across all five:** the retrieval layer of 2027 is a *policy* + *toolbox* + *observability contract*, not a pipeline. No observed source predicts a return to static pipelines.

### What died — narratives and evidence

**1. The fixed pipeline as *the* architecture (died ~mid-2025, publicly).** The strongest evidence is self-inflicted: LlamaIndex — whose 2023 identity *was* the pipeline — published "RAG is dead, long live agentic retrieval" (May 29, 2025), stating "agentic strategies are table stakes." Chroma's CEO called the term itself incoherent (Aug 2025). Bustamante's Obituary (Oct 2025; 290 HN points, 179 comments — the highest-engagement practitioner post on the topic observed this session) generalized from building a legal-search product: agents + context windows kill chunk-embed-rerank. The counter-position exists and matters: Hamel Husain's "Stop Saying RAG Is Dead" (Jul 2025) and Qdrant's earlier "RAG is Dead. Long Live RAG" (Apr 2024) argue the *components* survive under new names. Both sides agree on the architecture-level fact: nobody defends static top-k stuffing as a default in 2026.

**2. "Long context kills retrieval" (died Jul 2025–Sep 2025).** The 2024 talking point that 1M-token windows obsolete retrieval did not survive contact with measurement: Chroma's Context Rot report (Jul 2025, 18 models, 194,480 calls) showed non-uniform degradation with input length even on trivial tasks. Ironically, the same period's sparse-attention cost collapse (DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp, −50%) strengthened the *economic* case for big contexts while the *accuracy* case was being dismantled — producing today's nuanced cache-vs-retrieve frontier rather than a winner.

**3. Prompt compression as a research line (quiet stall, ~2025).** Microsoft's LLMLingua (6.5k stars) was a 2023–24 darling; its repo news trail ends December 13, 2024 (observed Aug 2026). Inference: provider-side prompt caching, 10×-cheaper tokens, and sparse attention removed the economic niche; compaction/summarization inside agent loops (Anthropic canon; ACE's *deliberately anti-compression* stance — brevity bias is now a named failure mode) replaced it architecturally. Labeled architectural-inference: no maintainer statement of abandonment was observed.

**4. Trained adaptive-RAG controllers — Self-RAG/FLARE/CRAG-style (superseded as *frontier methods*, 2025).** The 2023–24 program of fine-tuning models with special reflection tokens or confidence critics to decide when/what to retrieve does not appear among the 2026 frontier *method* proposals surveyed here; the identical decision problem ("when should active RAG retrieve?") reappears as budget-aware *policy* evaluation (arXiv:2607.24010) and RL abstention rewards (arXiv:2607.10738). The mechanism moved from bespoke supervised token vocabularies into general tool-use RL. Architectural-inference.

*Cross-reference — narrow this claim to "not a frontier method," not "gone."* `advanced-rag-architectures.md` documents a live 2026 literature on exactly this lineage, and it is not in error: the line remains active (a) as an **object of audit and reproduction** — the open-source CRAG reproduction (arXiv:2603.16169, Mar 2026) rebuilds CRAG with Wikipedia + Phi-3-mini and shows via SHAP that its T5 evaluator keys on named-entity alignment rather than semantic relevance; FlashRAG standardization and the Moskvoretskii uncertainty baselines play the same role — and (b) as a **persisting decision problem under new framing**: arXiv:2607.24010 explicitly describes Active RAG as "a budget-sensitive case of agentic RAG and self-adaptive retrieval," i.e. continuity rather than abandonment. Its finding that "simple uncertainty or retrieval-score baselines often rival learned utility routers" is also independent 2026 support for that file's deflation thesis. So the two files agree on substance — no one proposes new reflection-token controllers at the frontier — and differ only on what "absent" means: absent from *what to build next*, present in *what is being audited and reframed*.

**5. Embedding-index-first code retrieval (publicly rejected May 2025 → RL-confirmed May 2026).** Cline's post is an explicit product-level abandonment with three stated reasons (fragmentation, decay, IP liability); Anthropic's JIT-retrieval canon and Claude Skills institutionalized the alternative; GrepSeek then demonstrated RL-trained shell-command retrieval *outperforming* embedding baselines on open-domain QA. Caveat: this is contested, not universal — GrepSeek itself documents the lexical ceiling, and embedding-based code search still ships in some IDEs (not directly verified this session).

**6. GraphRAG universality (corrected Jun 2026).** The 2024 GraphRAG boom produced strong claims of general superiority; "Is GraphRAG Needed?" (arXiv:2606.25656) is the measured correction — "expanded retrieval does not proportionally improve generation quality," and context-optimized basic RAG matches graph pipelines at 19–53% lower token cost on semi-structured KBs. Graphs survive as a *conditional* tool (and thrive in new roles: CoEvoKG's agent-written graphs, ColGraphRAG's multimodal evidence graphs).

**7. The standalone vector-DB category (consolidating 2025–26, not dead).** Voyage AI (embeddings) absorbed by MongoDB for $220M (Feb 2025); Pinecone rearchitected twice then relaunched as "Nexus: The Knowledge Engine for Agents" (May 2026); Chroma's own CEO argues vector DBs are "only part of a larger retrieval system." No failure/sale reporting was observed for Pinecone in the HN record checked — this is repositioning under pressure, not death. Marked as consolidation with direct product evidence.

**8. The term "RAG" itself (deprecated in research titling, 2025→2026).** Observable in this session's arXiv listings: 2026 papers overwhelmingly title themselves "context engineering," "agentic search," "deep research," or "search agents" — e.g., a regulatory-compliance system that would have been an "enterprise RAG" paper in 2024 ships as "an evolving context engineering pipeline" (arXiv:2607.24791). The 1,400-paper survey (arXiv:2507.13334) formalizes the demotion: RAG is one of four "system implementations" of context engineering.

## Failure modes & critiques (frontier-specific)

1. **Effort–quality decoupling in search agents.** More searching ≠ better answers; accuracy tracks cumulative recall; agents lack sufficiency-based stopping (arXiv:2608.01913, verified). Framework implication: retrieval budgets and stop-conditions must be first-class, not emergent.
2. **Retrieval gaps vs utilization gaps are different diseases** with different cures (better acquisition vs better grounding/attention), yet current systems and evals conflate them (same source; echoed by "Before Reasoning Can Fail: Pre-Evidence Procedural Failures in Agentic RAG," arXiv:2608.02011 **[listing-only]**).
3. **Verification inertia.** Deep-research agents anchor on early evidence and under-verify against misleading sources (DRNOISE, arXiv:2607.17291 **[listing-only]**; "Is Deep Research Reliable?" arXiv:2607.20891 **[listing-only]**). Citation trustworthiness audits find existence/fidelity/applicability failures (arXiv:2607.20872 **[listing-only]**).
4. **Memory self-editing degenerates.** Brevity bias and context collapse (ACE, verified) mean naive "let the agent maintain its own notes" architectures erode their own knowledge.
5. **Self-evolution is a persistence layer for attacks.** SkillJack backdoors; benign-experience composition harms; authority/permission collapse at consolidation; MemArena's universal permission failure (mixed verified/listing-only). No observed system passes a provenance-preserving-consolidation test.
6. **Single-vector retrieval has representational ceilings** (LIMIT) — but the field partially over-corrected; MED theory (arXiv:2601.20844) shows the binding constraint is combinatorial top-k expressiveness, so bolting a bigger embedding on is neither necessary nor sufficient. Systems built in 2026 must justify *which* scoring function family they need, not pick a vendor default.
7. **KV-cache reuse is correctness- and security-fragile.** Position-shift errors on non-prefix reuse; "when is it safe to reuse an answer" is an open routing problem; caches leak via side channels (all **[listing-only]**, but three independent groups).
8. **Context rot bounds every "just add context" strategy** (verified, 18 models) — including CAG, long-horizon agent transcripts, and skill libraries loaded wholesale.
9. **Evaluation lag.** The strongest 2026 diagnostic work exists precisely because standard QA metrics saturated while long-horizon behavior stayed broken; budget-aware retrieval evaluation only appeared Jul 2026 (arXiv:2607.24010 **[listing-only]**). Vendor memory benchmarks (LOCOMO-based) remain self-reported.
10. **The sim-to-real gap in RL search training.** ZeroSearch-style simulated corpora cut cost but train against a model of the world; the 2026 correction back toward verified real-corpus environments concedes the problem.
11. **The lexical ceiling of index-free retrieval.** GrepSeek's own limitation statement: shell-command interaction fails under surface-form variation. The Cline/Anthropic anti-index stance therefore has a measured boundary — it works because reasoning models iterate on cheap tools, and it stops working where vocabulary mismatch dominates. Framework designs that pick a side (index-free *or* embedding-first) inherit the losing regime of the other.
12. **Multimodal/visual retrieval cost.** ColPali-lineage systems multiply storage and scoring cost per document page; the entire 2026 compression race (MarginMerge, token merging, patch compression, any-modality index compression) exists because naive multi-vector storage is economically prohibitive at enterprise scale — an unsolved systems tax on the representational fix for LIMIT.
13. **Amplified misuse.** SciHazard's finding that deep-research agents produce 32.3% higher decomposed-harm scores than plain LLMs means retrieval capability itself is a safety surface; retrieval frameworks have no equivalent of a content-safety layer today.

## Open problems (seeds for a next-generation framework)

1. **Evidence-sufficiency estimation as a first-class primitive.** No deployed system can answer "do I now hold enough evidence, at what confidence, to stop retrieving?" — the empirically dominant failure axis (2608.01913). A framework should expose sufficiency/coverage state, not just top-k lists.
2. **Separating and instrumenting retrieval-gap vs utilization-gap.** Observability that attributes a wrong answer to acquisition vs grounding vs synthesis — Bousetouane's seven context-quality criteria are a starting taxonomy but lack agreed measurement.
3. **Provenance- and permission-preserving memory consolidation.** Every consolidation/summarization step currently strips source authority (Authority Collapse; MemArena). Needed: memory writes as typed, signed, ACL-carrying objects that survive compression — no observed system does this.
4. **Trust boundaries for self-evolving stores.** Skill/experience libraries need admission control, quarantine, and rollback against SkillJack-class persistence attacks; "evolution" and "immutability of safety constraints" must be reconciled architecturally.
5. **Principled representation selection.** Given MED = Θ(k) and LIMIT, the choice among single-vector / multi-vector / lexical / generative scoring should be derivable from query-combinatorics of the workload, not fashion. An adaptive framework could route by predicted relevance-structure complexity — grep for surface-form precision, late interaction for compositional recall — making the GrepSeek-conceded gap the router's objective.
6. **KV-state as a managed retrieval artifact.** Unify CAG and RAG: a cache-aware planner that decides materialize/reuse/invalidate per knowledge segment, with formal safe-reuse conditions (extending Grounded Cache Routing) and side-channel isolation. Nobody has published a framework-level treatment.
7. **Credit assignment and cost-awareness in retrieval policies.** Turn-level attribution (LOTAPO-style) plus explicit token/latency budgets in both training rewards and evaluation (2607.24010) — currently bolted on, never co-designed with the retrieval substrate.
8. **Anti-inertia verification.** Mechanisms that force disconfirming search against misleading evidence (DRNOISE) — plausibly a retrieval-framework feature (adversarial second-pass retrieval) rather than a model feature.

### Design implications distilled for the next-generation framework

Reading the eight problems together, the frontier evidence points at a framework whose contract differs from 2024 RAG libraries in kind, not degree:

- **The unit of abstraction is a *retrieval policy with a budget*, not a retriever.** Every 2026 result about stopping, abstention, credit assignment, and budget-aware evaluation concerns decisions the 2024 abstractions cannot even express. The framework API should accept a task + budget + sufficiency target and expose the policy's evidence-state, tool choices, and stop rationale.
- **The toolbox is heterogeneous by design.** Empirically validated channels as of mid-2026: grep/shell (GrepSeek), fielded boolean (2608.02751), single-vector ANN (bounded but cheap), late-interaction/multi-vector (LIMIT-escaping, expensive), graph (conditional per 2606.25656), generative retrieval (ambiguity-fragile per 2604.05764), and cached KV segments (CAG regime). No observed source justifies shipping only one.
- **Memory writes are a trust boundary.** SkillJack, experience-composition harms, and authority collapse jointly imply admission control, provenance-typed records, ACL-preserving consolidation, and rollback — none observed in any shipped memory system (MemArena's universal permission failure is the measurement).
- **Observability must attribute failures to acquisition vs utilization** (2608.01913's gap taxonomy; Bousetouane's seven criteria) — otherwise every downstream eval conflates retriever bugs with grounding bugs, which is precisely the 2024-era evaluation pathology the deep-research benchmarks exposed.
- **Cache management is part of retrieval, not serving.** The 2026 KV-reuse literature (safe-reuse routing, position-agnostic encoding, side-channel isolation) implies the framework should plan *which knowledge lives as reusable KV state vs which is fetched per-query* — a decision no current RAG framework models.
- **Progressive disclosure is the loading discipline.** Skills/MCP-code-execution demonstrated ~98% context savings by loading references first and content on demand; a framework should make reference-first loading the default and full materialization the exception.
