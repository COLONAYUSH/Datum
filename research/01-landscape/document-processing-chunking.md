# Document Ingestion, Parsing & Chunking for RAG — Research Landscape (as of August 2026)

## Scope

This document surveys the *first mile* of RAG and agentic-memory systems: converting raw
documents (PDF, HTML, Office, scans, code, tables) into retrievable units. It covers:

1. **Parsing engines** — pipeline-based (GROBID, Unstructured, Docling, MinerU, marker),
   end-to-end neural (Nougat, olmOCR, Mistral OCR), and general vision-LLM parsing.
2. **Layout analysis, reading order, table/figure extraction** and the benchmarks that
   evaluate them (OmniDocBench, olmOCR-Bench, OHR-Bench).
3. **Chunking** — fixed/recursive, semantic, LLM/perplexity-based (Meta-Chunking,
   LumberChunker, MoC), late chunking, contextual retrieval / chunk headers,
   proposition-level indexing, hierarchical/parent-document (RAPTOR, dsRAG, RDR2),
   page-level, cross-granularity (MoG, FreeChunker), and structure-aware chunking for
   code (cAST) and tables.
4. **What controlled evaluations actually show** about chunking's impact (Chroma, NVIDIA,
   NAACL/COLING/2026 arXiv ablations) — including strong null results.
5. **Metadata enrichment, deduplication, incremental ingestion.**
6. **Alternatives that dissolve the parsing/chunking problem** — visual retrieval
   (ColPali) and reasoning-over-structure retrieval (PageIndex).

Emphasis: failure modes, contradictory evidence, and open problems that motivate a
next-generation ingestion layer. Sources are labeled: [peer-reviewed], [preprint],
[vendor], [practitioner]. All citations were verified against fetched pages or search
results; claims I could not verify are marked *uncertain*.

## Lineage & chronological development

**Phase 1 — Scholarly-document IE (2008–2022).** GROBID (Lopez, ongoing since ~2008)
established the pipeline paradigm: CRF (later optional deep-learning) sequence labeling
over "layout tokens" extracted via pdfalto, producing structured TEI/XML for scientific
papers; still in production at Semantic Scholar, ResearchGate, HAL, CERN, processing
~10.6 PDF/s on a 16-core machine ([GROBID GitHub](https://github.com/kermitt2/grobid)).
Layout-detection datasets (PubLayNet, DocLayNet) and table-structure models (TableFormer)
matured in this era, mostly at IBM Research and academia.

**Phase 2 — End-to-end neural document conversion (2023).** Nougat — Blecher et al.
(Meta AI) — arXiv 2308.13418 — 2023 — a vision transformer that converts scientific PDFs
directly to markup, preserving math. It demonstrated end-to-end conversion but is
scientific-document-specific; repetition/degeneration failure loops on out-of-domain
pages are widely reported by practitioners (the abstract page I fetched does not detail
this; the limitation is consistent across practitioner benchmarks — *moderately
uncertain in exact magnitude*).

**Phase 3 — RAG-era chunking heuristics (2022–2023).** LangChain's
`RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter` and token splitters became the de-facto default;
"semantic chunking" (embedding-similarity boundary detection, popularized by Greg
Kamradt) and parent-document/small-to-big retrieval entered framework toolkits. Almost
none of this was validated by controlled experiments at the time.

**Phase 4 — Retrieval-granularity research (Dec 2023–2024).**
- *Dense X Retrieval: What Retrieval Granularity Should We Use?* — Chen et al. —
  EMNLP 2024 main / arXiv 2312.06648 — introduced **propositions** (atomic,
  self-contained factoids generated by a fine-tuned "Propositionizer") as the retrieval
  unit; proposition indexing beat passage/sentence units on open-domain QA retrieval
  [peer-reviewed].
- *RAPTOR* — Sarthi et al. — ICLR 2024 / arXiv 2401.18059 — recursive
  embed→cluster→summarize tree; retrieval across abstraction levels; +20% absolute on
  QuALITY with GPT-4 [peer-reviewed].
- *LumberChunker* — Duarte et al. — arXiv 2406.17526 — 2024 — iterative LLM prompting to
  find content-shift points in narrative books; +7.37% DCG@20 over baselines on its own
  GutenQA benchmark [preprint; self-benchmark caveat].
- *Late Chunking* — Günther et al. (Jina AI/Weaviate) — arXiv 2409.04701 — 2024 (v3
  2025) — embed the whole document with a long-context embedding model, chunk *after*
  the transformer, before pooling; chunk vectors inherit bidirectional document context
  with no extra storage or LLM calls [preprint].
- *Contextual Retrieval* — Anthropic — Sept 2024 engineering blog — prepend 50–100
  tokens of LLM-generated document context to each chunk before embedding + BM25;
  reported failed-retrieval reduction 35% (embeddings only), 49% (+contextual BM25),
  67% (+reranking), at ~$1.02 per million document tokens with prompt caching
  [vendor, influential].
- *Meta-Chunking* — Zhao et al. — arXiv 2410.12788 — 2024/2025 — "meta-chunk"
  granularity between sentence and paragraph; Margin Sampling Chunking and
  perplexity(PPL)-based chunking with small LMs [preprint].
- First strong **null result**: *Is Semantic Chunking Worth the Computational Cost?* —
  Qu, Tu, Bao — arXiv 2410.13070 — NAACL 2025 Findings (per multiple secondary
  sources) — across document retrieval, evidence retrieval, and answer generation,
  semantic chunking's costs "are not justified by consistent performance gains" vs
  fixed-size chunking [peer-reviewed].

**Phase 5 — Parsing industrialization + VLM parsing (2024–2025).**
- *Docling* — IBM Research (Auer et al.) — arXiv 2408.09869 — MIT-licensed pipeline:
  DocLayNet layout model + TableFormer table structure + reading-order recovery on
  commodity hardware [preprint + OSS].
- *OmniDocBench* — Ouyang et al. — CVPR 2025 / arXiv 2412.07626 — 1,651 pages, 9 document
  sources, component-level matching metrics for text/tables/formulas/reading order;
  became the standard parsing leaderboard (MinerU, Mistral OCR, olmOCR, MinerU2-VLM all
  evaluated there) [peer-reviewed].
- *OHR-Bench ("OCR Hinders RAG")* — Zhang et al. — ICCV 2025 / arXiv 2412.02592 — 8,561
  document images, 8,498 QA pairs across 7 domains; **no current OCR solution is
  competent for building high-quality RAG knowledge bases**; even the best loses ~14%
  F1 end-to-end vs ground-truth text; distinguishes Semantic vs Formatting noise
  [peer-reviewed].
- *olmOCR* — Poznanski et al. (Allen AI) — arXiv 2502.18443 — 2025 — fine-tuned 7B VLM
  ("document anchoring" on 260k pages) producing linearized natural-reading-order text;
  claims $176/million pages vs $6,240+ for GPT-4o, and beats GPT-4o/Gemini Flash 2/
  Qwen2.5-VL on its own olmOCR-Bench; swapping GROBID+rules for olmOCR in LM pretraining
  gave +1.3pp on downstream benchmarks [preprint + OSS].
- *Mistral OCR* — Mistral — March 2025 — claimed 94.89% accuracy vs GPT-4o 89.77%,
  Google Doc AI 83.42%; 2,000 pages/min/node; $1/1,000 pages. Notably the announcement
  page now carries a deprecation notice ("no longer maintained, replaced by our latest
  OCR models") — vendor OCR is a fast-churn commodity [vendor].
- Practitioner shift to generic VLMs: "Ingesting Millions of PDFs and why Gemini 2.0
  Changes Everything" — Filimonov — Jan 2025 — thesis that frontier-VLM parsing became
  orders of magnitude cheaper, making OCR-pipeline engineering optional for many
  workloads (I verified title/date; specific cost-per-page numbers in the body not
  re-verified) [practitioner, *details uncertain*].
- *ColPali* — Faysse et al. — ICLR 2025 / arXiv 2407.01449 — bypass parsing entirely:
  embed page *images* with a VLM into multi-vector (late-interaction) representations;
  outperforms text-extraction pipelines on ViDoRe while being "drastically simpler"
  [peer-reviewed].

**Phase 6 — Adaptive granularity, structure-native retrieval, and disillusionment
(2025–2026).**
- *Mix-of-Granularity (MoG/MoGG)* — Zhong et al. — COLING 2025 — query-conditioned
  router selects granularity level; graph extension retrieves distant snippets
  [peer-reviewed].
- *MoC: Mixtures of Text Chunking Learners* — Zhao et al. — arXiv 2503.09600 — 2025 —
  router picks among chunkers; chunkers emit chunking regexes rather than full
  rewrites [preprint].
- *cAST* — Zhang et al. — EMNLP 2025 Findings / arXiv 2506.15655 — AST-based recursive
  split-then-merge chunking for code; +4.3 Recall@5 on RepoEval, +2.67 Pass@1 on
  SWE-bench [peer-reviewed].
- *FreeChunker* — Zhang et al. — arXiv 2510.20356 — 2025/2026 — sentences as atomic
  units; retrieval assembles arbitrary sentence combinations, eliminating boundary
  detection [preprint].
- *RDR2 (Retrieve–DocumentRoute–Read)* — Xu et al. — arXiv 2510.04293 — 2025 — LLM
  router navigates document structure trees during retrieval instead of treating chunks
  as isolated [preprint].
- 2026 ablation wave (see "What the evidence says"): repeated findings that simple
  recursive/fixed/page-level chunking matches or beats sophisticated methods, while
  parsing/OCR quality and embedding-model choice dominate outcomes.
- *When Good OCR Is Not Enough* — Sun et al. — arXiv 2605.00911 — 2026 — high
  WER/CER-level OCR accuracy does **not** predict RAG performance; structural/semantic
  errors cause retrieval failures even at low character error rates [preprint].
- Vectorless/structure-native retrieval matures: PageIndex (Vectify AI) — hierarchical
  tree index + LLM reasoning navigation, "no chunking, no vector DB"; claims 98.7% on
  FinanceBench via Mafin 2.5 [vendor, self-reported].

## State of the art — mid-2026 snapshot

**Parsing.** Practice has bifurcated. (a) *Pipeline parsers* — Docling (open, strongest
open-source table extraction; 97.9% cell accuracy in the Procycons sustainability-report
benchmark), MinerU/MinerU2-VLM, marker (Surya-based; 76.0% on olmocr-bench in balanced
mode at 2.9 pages/s; claims to beat MinerU and Docling on speed+accuracy simultaneously
[vendor-adjacent]), Unstructured (broadest format coverage, weaker structural precision
— 75% on complex tables, slowest per-page in Procycons). (b) *VLM parsers* — olmOCR,
Mistral OCR successors, GLM-OCR, POINTS-Reader, plus direct frontier-VLM parsing
(Gemini/Claude/GPT). OmniDocBench is the reference benchmark; the consistent finding
across it and independent tests (f22labs, IntuitionLabs, Firecrawl 2026 roundups) is
**no parser wins across document types** — clean digital PDFs, scans, tables, formulas,
multilingual, and handwriting each have different winners.

**Chunking.** The evidence-based default in 2026 is embarrassingly simple:
recursive/fixed splitting at ~400–512 tokens (Firecrawl 2026 guidance; Chroma's
token-level evaluation; NAACL 2025 null result on semantic chunking; Vecta's Feb-2026
benchmark where recursive-512 scored 69% vs semantic chunking's 54%), or **page-level
chunking** (NVIDIA's five-dataset study: 0.648 mean answer accuracy, lowest variance).
The methods with credible measured gains are those that add *context* rather than find
*better boundaries*: contextual retrieval / contextual chunk headers (Anthropic; dsRAG
AutoContext), late chunking (Jina), hierarchical retrieval with segment reassembly
(RAPTOR; dsRAG RSE), and domain-structure-aware chunking where structure is real and
machine-readable (cAST for code; element-based chunking à la Unstructured `by_title`).

**The frontier has moved upstream and downstream of the chunker**: upstream to parsing
fidelity (OHR-Bench shows OCR noise costs more than chunking strategy gains) and
downstream to retrieval that is structure- or vision-native (ColPali-style visual
retrieval, PageIndex/RDR2-style tree navigation), both of which shrink the role of
chunking as an irreversible preprocessing commitment.

### End-to-end neural / VLM parsers

- **Nougat** (Meta AI, arXiv 2308.13418): first credible PDF→markup transformer;
  scientific docs only.
- **olmOCR** (Allen AI, arXiv 2502.18443): 7B fine-tuned VLM; "document anchoring"
  conditions generation on PDF-internal text/layout; $176/M pages self-hosted; released
  model+data+bench (CC-BY). olmOCR-Bench (1,400 PDFs, ~8,400 unit tests) is now a
  common yardstick (marker reports against it).
- **Mistral OCR** (vendor, Mar 2025): 94.89% claimed accuracy, $1/1k pages — already
  deprecated by its vendor within ~a year; independent tests (f22labs) found weakness
  on multilingual, tables, low-quality scans, contradicting launch claims.
- **Generic frontier VLMs** (Gemini/GPT/Claude/Qwen-VL): OmniDocBench and OHR-Bench both
  evaluate them; they are strong but hallucinate structure, are expensive at corpus
  scale, and (per OHR-Bench) still degrade RAG vs ground truth.
- **Specialized successors 2025–2026**: POINTS-Reader (arXiv 2509.01215,
  distillation-free VLM doc conversion), GLM-OCR (arXiv 2603.10910), UniRec-0.1B
  (arXiv 2512.21095, 0.1B unified text+formula recognition) — trend toward *small*
  specialized VLMs [preprints; seen in search results, not deeply reviewed].

### Benchmarks for parsing

- **OmniDocBench** (CVPR 2025, arXiv 2412.07626): component-segmentation + matching
  metrics over text/tables/formulas/reading-order; 9 sources incl. handwriting and
  newspapers; explicitly motivated by prior benchmarks' "narrow coverage and simplified,
  unrealistic evaluation."
- **olmOCR-Bench**: unit-test style (does output contain/order specific facts), robust
  to markup variance.
- **OHR-Bench** (ICCV 2025): the only benchmark measuring parsing quality by *downstream
  RAG* effect. Headline: best OCR still −14% F1 vs ground truth; formatting noise and
  semantic noise degrade retrieval and generation differently.
- **When Good OCR Is Not Enough** (arXiv 2605.00911, 2026): 11 industrial document
  types; WER/CER do not predict RAG outcomes; failure is category-dependent and appears
  on both retrieval and generation sides.
- **SO-Bench** (arXiv 2511.21750): structured-output evaluation of multimodal LLMs
  [seen in results; not reviewed in depth].

## Chunking — taxonomy and key work

### 1. Fixed / recursive / sentence splitting
Character-, token-, or separator-hierarchy splitting with optional overlap. Still the
strongest cost-adjusted baseline in nearly every controlled study (Chroma; NAACL 2025;
Vecta-via-Firecrawl 2026; arXiv 2607.01852). Chroma found library **defaults** (e.g.,
800 tokens/400 overlap) perform poorly — tuning matters more than method.

### 2. Semantic chunking (embedding-boundary detection)
Kamradt-style discontinuity detection; ClusterSemanticChunker (Chroma) uses dynamic
programming to maximize intra-chunk similarity. Evidence is genuinely split:
- **Against**: arXiv 2410.13070 (NAACL 2025 Findings) — no consistent gains; Vecta
  Feb-2026 — semantic chunking 54% vs recursive-512 69%, producing 43-token fragments;
  arXiv 2607.01852 (2026, academic theses) — cluster-based did not beat simpler
  strategies, and RAGAS faithfulness proved unreliable as a measuring instrument.
- **For**: Chroma's ClusterSemanticChunker got best precision/IoU (8.0%/8.0% at 200
  tokens) with recall ≈ recursive; an MDPI Bioengineering clinical-decision-support
  study (Nov 2025, via search snippet — *details uncertain*) reported 87% vs 13% for
  fixed-size when chunks align to topic boundaries; several ACM/ACL 2025 papers
  (Semantic Layout Chunking, Recursive Semantic) report gains on their own setups.
- **Synthesis**: gains appear when (a) documents have strong real topical structure,
  (b) the baseline is untuned, or (c) evaluation rewards precision over recall.
  On generic corpora with tuned baselines, gains evaporate.

### 3. LLM- and perplexity-based chunking
- **LumberChunker** (arXiv 2406.17526): iterative LLM boundary detection; +7.37% DCG@20
  on GutenQA (narrative books). Cost: one LLM pass per group of passages.
- **Meta-Chunking** (arXiv 2410.12788): margin-sampling and PPL-based boundary scoring
  with *small* LMs — attacks the cost objection directly.
- **MoC** (arXiv 2503.09600): router over multiple chunkers; chunkers output regexes to
  extract chunks, avoiding LLM copy-generation cost/hallucination.
- Chroma's LLMChunker: highest recall (91.9%) but poor precision (3.9%) — LLM boundaries
  are not automatically efficient boundaries.

### 4. Late chunking (representation-level fix)
arXiv 2409.04701 (Jina). Chunk *after* the transformer: every chunk embedding is
conditioned bidirectionally on the whole document (up to the 8k context of the
embedder). No storage overhead, no LLM calls. Jina's Part-II blog makes two important
claims: (i) late chunking is **resilient to bad boundaries** — fixed-token boundaries
+ late chunking beat semantic boundaries + naive embedding, arguing boundary-finding
and context-preservation are near-orthogonal problems; (ii) honest negative result:
"not a single instance where a weaker model with late chunking outperforms a stronger
model without it" — embedding model quality dominates. Limitations: requires
long-context embedders; document length capped by embedder context; per-chunk vectors
still pooled (contrast late interaction).

### 5. Contextual retrieval / contextual chunk headers (context-augmentation)
- **Anthropic Contextual Retrieval** (Sept 2024, vendor): prepend 50–100 tokens of
  LLM-written situating context; −35/−49/−67% failed retrievals (embeddings / +BM25 /
  +rerank); ~$1.02/M doc tokens with caching. Also notable for its anti-RAG threshold:
  under ~200k tokens, skip retrieval and stuff the context window.
- **dsRAG AutoContext / contextual chunk headers** (GitHub, D-Star AI): document- and
  section-level headers prepended before embedding; combined with LLM "semantic
  sectioning" and Relevant Segment Extraction (query-time merging of adjacent chunks
  into variable-length segments). Self-reported: FinanceBench 96.6% vs 32% vanilla RAG;
  KITE 8.42/10 vs 4.72 baseline [vendor-adjacent OSS, self-benchmark].
- KX/Medium analysis frames late chunking vs contextual retrieval as the same math
  (conditioning chunk representations on document context) at very different cost points.

### 6. Proposition / atomic-fact indexing
**Dense X Retrieval** (Chen et al., EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2312.06648): propositions as
retrieval units beat passages/sentences for dense retrieval and downstream QA at fixed
token budgets; released FactoidWiki. Costs/critiques: corpus-wide LLM generation
(Propositionizer) — expensive, lossy for context-dependent content (tables, narrative,
code), index size blow-up, and decontextualization errors compound silently. Successor
idea: "Evidence Units" (arXiv 2604.00500, 2026) — ontology-grounded units aiming at
parser-independent retrieval [preprint, seen not reviewed].

### 7. Hierarchical / parent-document / tree
- **RAPTOR** (ICLR 2024): retrieval over recursive summary trees; strong on multi-hop
  and holistic questions (QuALITY +20% absolute w/ GPT-4). Cost: LLM summarization of
  the whole corpus; summaries can hallucinate and go stale.
- **Parent-document / small-to-big** (LangChain/LlamaIndex pattern): embed small, return
  big. Ubiquitous in practice; little controlled published evidence isolating its gain.
- **RDR2** (arXiv 2510.04293): trainable LLM router navigating document structure trees
  at retrieval time — structure kept, not flattened.
- **KohakuRAG** (arXiv 2603.07612): hierarchical document indexing framework [seen].
- **PageIndex** (Vectify): tree ToC + LLM navigation; "similarity ≠ relevance";
  no chunking at all. Vendor-claimed 98.7% FinanceBench.

### 8. Page-level and layout-unit chunking
NVIDIA's five-dataset study (developer blog): page-level chunking best mean accuracy
(0.648) and lowest variance (0.107) vs token-based (128–2048) and section-level;
extreme sizes lose; factoid queries prefer small chunks, analytical prefer large.
Pages are a natural unit because they align with (a) parser output, (b) visual
retrieval (ColPali), (c) human citations. Weakness: pages are typographic accidents —
sections straddle page breaks.

### 9. Cross-granularity / adaptive
- **Mix-of-Granularity** (COLING 2025): router picks granularity per query; MoGG graph
  variant reaches distant snippets.
- **FreeChunker** (arXiv 2510.20356): sentences as atoms, arbitrary sentence-set
  retrieval; single forward pass yields multi-granularity embeddings; claims better
  retrieval and time-efficiency on LongBench V2.
- **ChunkRAG** (NAACL SRW 2025, arXiv 2410.19572): post-retrieval LLM filtering at
  chunk level to cut hallucination.
- These converge on one thesis: **fixing granularity at index time is the mistake**;
  granularity should be resolved at query time.

### 10. Structure-aware chunking for code and tables
- **cAST** (EMNLP 2025 Findings, arXiv 2506.15655): recursively split large AST nodes,
  greedily merge siblings under a size budget; language-agnostic via tree-sitter-style
  parsing; +4.3 Recall@5 RepoEval, +2.67 Pass@1 SWE-bench. The cleanest demonstration
  that when a domain has a true grammar, chunking on it beats text heuristics.
- Tables: "Structure-Aware Chunking for Complex Tables in RAG" (Emerging Science
  Journal 2025/2026 [seen]); practice-side techniques: keep tables atomic, serialize to
  Markdown/HTML, repeat headers per row-group chunk, LLM table summaries as retrieval
  proxies (parser support: Docling TableFormer, marker VLM fallback, Unstructured
  Table elements). Benchmarks show tables remain a top parsing failure mode
  (OmniDocBench; HCT-QA arXiv 2504.20047 for human-centric tables).
- Conversation/memory chunking: SeCom (arXiv 2502.05589) applies segmentation ideas to
  conversational memory construction — evidence the chunking question recurs in agent
  memory, not just documents.
- Multimodal: "Chunking Strategies for Multimodal AI Systems" (arXiv 2512.00185)
  [seen; not reviewed].

## What the evidence actually says about chunking impact

Synthesis of the controlled studies (Chroma 2024; NVIDIA; NAACL 2025 arXiv 2410.13070;
COLING 2025 MoG; arXiv 2606.00881; arXiv 2607.01852; Vecta-via-Firecrawl 2026):

1. **Chunking matters, but boundedly.** Chroma: up to ~9% recall spread between
   strategies; badly tuned defaults are the main hazard. This is a real but second-order
   effect compared to parsing quality (OHR-Bench: −14%+ F1 from OCR alone) and
   embedding-model choice (Jina's "stronger model without late chunking beats weaker
   with").
2. **Sophistication does not reliably pay.** Semantic chunking loses or draws vs tuned
   recursive splitting in 3 of 4 independent evaluations (2410.13070, 2607.01852,
   Vecta; Chroma is the partial exception on precision). LLM chunking buys recall,
   not precision, at large cost.
3. **Smaller-than-default chunks (≈200–512 tokens) with little/no overlap** are
   repeatedly optimal for retrieval metrics (Chroma); **page-level** is the robust
   choice for end-to-end answer accuracy across heterogeneous corpora (NVIDIA). These
   two results are not contradictory — they optimize different metrics (token-precision
   vs answer accuracy with modern long-context readers).
4. **Context augmentation > boundary optimization.** The largest verified end-to-end
   gains in this space come from adding document context to chunks (Anthropic −67%
   failure w/ rerank; dsRAG's self-reported FinanceBench jump), not from moving
   boundaries.
5. **Evaluation is itself broken.** Most chunking papers evaluate on synthetic QA over
   clean text (skipping parsing), use position-insensitive metrics, or self-created
   benchmarks (LumberChunker/GutenQA, dsRAG/KITE). arXiv 2607.01852 found RAGAS
   faithfulness unreliable. arXiv 2606.00881 (2026) states chunking is "commonly
   treated as a simple preprocessing step" while introducing "impactful and often
   overlooked issues." Query-type dependence (factoid vs analytical, NVIDIA) is almost
   never controlled.

## Metadata, dedup, incremental ingestion

- **Metadata extraction/enrichment.** Production pattern: attach doc-level metadata
  (title, section path, page, date, source ACLs) to every chunk; contextual chunk
  headers (dsRAG) and Anthropic-style generated context are the LLM-era version.
  Unstructured's typed-element model (Title, NarrativeText, Table, PageBreak) is the
  most principled OSS substrate — chunking over elements, not characters, with
  `by_title` section preservation and `max/new_after_n_chars` budgets (Unstructured
  docs). Little *published research* isolates metadata's contribution — a genuine gap.
- **Deduplication.** Frameworks do exact/hash dedup: LlamaIndex ingestion pipeline
  keeps a `doc_id → document_hash` map; unchanged hash → skip, changed → reprocess and
  upsert (LlamaIndex docs). Near-duplicate and cross-version dedup (the hard case:
  v2 of a policy PDF vs v1) is essentially unaddressed in the RAG literature —
  practitioners report retrieval slots wasted on near-identical chunks; no standard
  benchmark exists. (Pretraining-corpus work like SemDeDup is the nearest analog —
  not verified here, flagged as background only.)
- **Incremental ingestion.** Hash-based upsert (LlamaIndex) handles document-level
  change, but chunk-level effects are unsolved: one edited paragraph shifts all
  downstream fixed-size boundaries, invalidating embeddings for the whole tail of a
  document; hierarchical/summary indexes (RAPTOR) and contextual chunks (Anthropic)
  are worse — an edit invalidates ancestor summaries / all chunk contexts. No published
  system I found does *stable-boundary* chunking (content-defined chunking à la rsync)
  for RAG — an obvious open problem.

## Failure modes & critiques

**Parsing layer**
1. **OCR/parsing noise is the dominant, silent ceiling.** OHR-Bench (ICCV 2025): no OCR
   solution is adequate; ≥14% F1 loss cascades through retrieval and generation.
   Mixedbread's follow-up: 67.6% answer accuracy with standard OCR vs 91.2% with
   perfect text. Most chunking papers never touch a real PDF, so the literature
   systematically overstates chunking's relative importance.
2. **Character-level metrics are the wrong target.** arXiv 2605.00911: low WER/CER
   coexists with severe RAG degradation; structural errors (merged columns, lost
   headers, broken reading order) matter more than character errors.
3. **VLM parsers hallucinate confidently.** Unlike pipeline omissions, VLM errors are
   fluent and plausible (fabricated table cells, invented totals); no parser today
   emits calibrated per-span confidence usable downstream.
4. **Tables and multi-column layouts remain unsolved in the tail.** LlamaParse: 100%
   numeric extraction but 0% table *placement* (Procycons) — values divorced from
   structure are worse than missing for QA. Multi-column interleaving breaks retrieval.
5. **Vendor churn and unreproducible claims.** Mistral OCR launched with SOTA claims
   (94.89%) in Mar 2025 and was vendor-deprecated within ~a year; several leaderboard
   claims (Reducto's LongExtractBench, dsRAG, PageIndex, Mixedbread) are self-hosted
   or self-benchmarked. Treat all vendor numbers as advertising priors.
6. **Reading order and provenance are afterthoughts.** Markdown output destroys
   bounding boxes/page coordinates unless explicitly preserved (Docling JSON does;
   most pipelines drop them), crippling citation, highlighting, and audit.

**Chunking layer**
7. **Index-time granularity commitment is architecturally wrong.** Every fixed choice
   is optimal only for a query distribution unknown at ingest (NVIDIA: factoid vs
   analytical want opposite sizes). MoG/FreeChunker/RSE all exist because of this.
8. **Semantic chunking is largely a null result** outside narrow conditions
   (2410.13070; 2607.01852; Vecta) — yet remains default advice in tutorials; the
   field's folk wisdom lags its own evidence by ~18 months.
9. **Context loss at boundaries** (anaphora, unit definitions, table headers) is the
   real disease; boundary-placement methods treat the symptom. Late chunking and
   contextual retrieval treat the disease, at opposite cost points.
10. **Enrichment methods don't compose cheaply.** Contextual retrieval invalidates on
    edit; RAPTOR summaries go stale; proposition indexes multiply storage and bake in
    Propositionizer hallucinations that are undetectable at query time.
11. **Chunking evaluation is unreliable.** Self-made benchmarks, synthetic queries over
    clean text, position-insensitive metrics, unreliable LLM-judge frameworks
    (RAGAS faithfulness per 2607.01852). There is no OHR-Bench-equivalent that jointly
    varies parser × chunker × retriever on real documents.
12. **Incremental update is unsupported by design.** Fixed-size boundaries are unstable
    under edits; hierarchical and contextualized indexes have O(document) or worse
    re-embedding blast radius. Framework support stops at file-hash upserts
    (LlamaIndex). Near-duplicate/version dedup is unstudied.
13. **Chunking research ignores non-prose.** cAST covers code; tables, spreadsheets,
    slides, forms, and mixed-modality documents lack validated chunking strategies;
    "atomic table" advice fails for 50-page financial tables.

## Open problems (seeds for a next-generation framework)

1. **Late-binding granularity.** Store atomic units (sentences/elements/propositions
   where reliable) plus structure; materialize retrieval units at query time
   conditioned on the query (generalize FreeChunker + MoG + RSE). What is the minimal
   index that supports arbitrary-granularity retrieval at near-vector-DB latency?
2. **Structure as a first-class index, not a serialization casualty.** Parsers already
   produce trees (Docling JSON, Unstructured elements, ASTs, ToCs); retrieval flattens
   them to text. Unify RDR2/PageIndex-style tree navigation with dense retrieval —
   when does reasoning-over-structure beat similarity, and can a router learn the
   choice per query?
3. **Uncertainty-carrying ingestion.** Parsers should emit per-span confidence and
   alternative hypotheses (n-best tables, ambiguous reading orders); retrieval and
   generation should consume them (abstain, re-OCR, or cite pixels). Nothing in the
   current stack propagates parsing uncertainty — OHR-Bench shows the cost of ignoring
   it.
4. **Joint parser×chunker×retriever benchmark on real documents** with edit streams:
   extend OHR-Bench with (a) chunking as a controlled variable, (b) query-type strata,
   (c) incremental-update episodes, (d) position-sensitive, non-LLM-judge metrics.
   Without it, chunking claims remain unfalsifiable in deployment terms.
5. **Stable-boundary / content-defined chunking for incremental corpora.** Boundaries
   that are invariant under local edits (cf. content-defined chunking in storage
   systems) would bound re-embedding blast radius; combine with hash-based upsert and
   summary-tree patching. Unexplored in the literature I found.
6. **Cheap contextualization.** Anthropic-style context works but costs an LLM pass per
   chunk and breaks on edit; late chunking is free but capped by embedder context and
   quality. Open: hierarchical/streaming late chunking beyond 8k, template- or
   structure-derived headers (section path + entity table) that approximate LLM context
   at ~zero cost, and measuring how much of the −67% comes from metadata vs prose.
7. **Text-extraction-free RAG at corpus scale.** ColPali-style visual retrieval removes
   the parsing ceiling for retrieval (Mixedbread: recovered ~70% of OCR-lost accuracy)
   but leaves generation reading pixels, storage-heavy multi-vectors, and no
   text-level provenance. Open: hybrid indexes that retrieve visually but ground/cite
   textually, and when each modality should be authoritative.
8. **Dedup and version reconciliation as retrieval semantics.** Near-duplicate chunks
   and document versions should be modeled (supersedes/duplicates edges), not just
   filtered; retrieval should return the *current* fact with its history available.
   No benchmark or standard method exists.
