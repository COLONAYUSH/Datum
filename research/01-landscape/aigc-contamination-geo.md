## Scope

This file covers the reflexive, ecosystem-level threat surface that most RAG literature treats as out of scope: **what happens to retrieval when the corpus itself is substantially produced by, and optimized against, language models.** Seven threads:

1. **Source bias** — the measured preference of neural retrievers and rerankers for LLM-generated text over semantically equivalent human text, and the still-unresolved dispute about its cause.
2. **Feedback loops** — spiral-of-silence dynamics in open-domain QA, escalation in recommender feedback loops, and simulated retrieval collapse under web-scale AI pollution.
3. **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** and its adversarial siblings — content crafted to be preferentially retrieved and cited by answer engines, from the original black-box optimization framing through 2026's attack/defense/benchmark literature, plus the strong null result from the field's first critical survey.
4. **Corpus poisoning** as the adversarial limit case of contamination, and why 2026 work says the classic attacks weaken in realistic pipelines.
5. **Detection, watermarking, provenance** — and the honest finding that this leg is the weakest, with detectors that are biased and paraphrase-fragile, and with the RAG watermarking literature solving a *different* problem (corpus IP theft) than the one contamination requires.
6. **Web-scale ecosystem effects** — crawler blocking, measured access asymmetry between reputable and low-quality sources, and publishers' strategic response.
7. **Collapse discourse** — model collapse, knowledge collapse, and the counter-evidence wave, because a next-generation framework should not be motivated by a claim the field is actively walking back.

Adjacent files cover retrieval quality (`retrieval-reranking-fusion.md`), evaluation (`evaluation-benchmarks.md`) and production concerns (`production-industry.md`). This file deliberately does not re-derive prompt-injection-as-security; it covers poisoning only where it bears on *corpus composition*.

## Lineage & key work

### 2023 — the phenomenon is named

- **LLMs may Dominate Information Access: Neural Retrievers are Biased Towards LLM-Generated Texts** — Sunhao Dai, Yuqi Zhou, Liang Pang, Weihao Liu, Xiaolin Hu, Yong Liu, Xiao Zhang, Gang Wang, Jun Xu — arXiv:2310.20501 v1, 31 Oct 2023. Retitled **"Neural Retrievers are Biased Towards LLM-Generated Content"** by v3 (31 Jul 2024) and published at **KDD 2024** (peer-reviewed). Coins **source bias**: in a corpus mixing human and LLM-rewritten versions of the same documents, neural first-stage retrievers *and* second-stage rerankers systematically rank the LLM version higher. Attributed to text-compression effects — LLM text has "more focused semantics with less noise," making semantic matching easier. Contributes a plug-and-play debiased optimization constraint and two benchmarks. Framed by the authors as "a critical wake-up call to the IR community."
  - *Note on the seed brief:* the brief cites this as "Dai et al. SIGIR 2024." The paper is KDD 2024; the v1 title is the "LLMs may dominate information access" phrasing, which explains the confusion. The SIGIR 2024 paper in this lineage is Xu et al. below. Both versions were verified on the arXiv abstract pages this session.
  - *Limitations/critiques:* the compression/semantics explanation was directly contradicted by later work (§Mechanism dispute). The construction uses LLM *rewrites* of human documents, which controls semantics well but is not how real AIGC enters a corpus — real AI content is generated, not paraphrased, and often lower-quality.

- **Invisible Relevance Bias: Text-Image Retrieval Models Prefer AI-Generated Images** — Shicheng Xu, Danyang Hou, Liang Pang, Jingcheng Deng, Jun Xu, Huawei Shen, Xueqi Cheng — arXiv:2311.14084 — **SIGIR 2024** (peer-reviewed) — v1 Nov 2023, final May 2024. The cross-modal analogue: text-image retrievers rank AI-generated images above real photographs of equivalent relevance. Mechanism claim: AI-generated images "induce the image encoder to embed additional information into their representation," consistent across generated images regardless of content. Notes the self-reinforcing loop — more generated images in training intensifies the bias. Proposes debiasing training.
  - *Critique:* "invisible" is doing real work here — the bias is not explained by visual quality, but the paper's mechanism (an extra signal in the embedding) is descriptive rather than causal.

- **GEO: Generative Engine Optimization** — Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan, Ameet Deshpande — arXiv:2311.09735 — **KDD 2024** (peer-reviewed) — submitted 16 Nov 2023, final Jun 2024. Defines the problem: as generative engines replace ranked lists, creators lose control over how their content is surfaced. Provides a black-box optimization framework over content, formal *visibility* metrics, and **GEO-bench** (diverse user queries with relevant web sources). Headline claim: GEO "can boost visibility by up to 40% in generative engine responses," with effectiveness varying by domain.
  - *Limitations/critiques:* this is the single most-cited number in the whole GEO ecosystem and it is the one most aggressively qualified by later work — see Martinez (2026), which finds the gain "valid within its experimental setting but conditional on a source already being present in a fixed context," establishing "neither organic discoverability nor durable traffic effects."

- **Poisoning Retrieval Corpora by Injecting Adversarial Passages** — Zexuan Zhong et al. — arXiv:2310.19156 — Oct 2023 — **[listing-only]** — the foundational corpus-poisoning attack on dense retrieval via discrete token perturbation. Predates and underpins the RAG-specific poisoning wave.

### 2024 — feedback loops, adversarial SEO, benchmarks, and the consent crisis

- **Spiral of Silence: How is Large Language Model Killing Information Retrieval? — A Case Study on Open Domain Question Answering** — Xiaoyang Chen, Ben He, Hongyu Lin, Xianpei Han, Tianshu Wang, Boxi Cao, Le Sun, Yingfei Sun — arXiv:2404.10496 — **ACL 2024** (peer-reviewed) — Apr–Jun 2024. Iteratively simulates a RAG ecosystem in which LLM output is fed back into the retrievable corpus. Finds LLM text consistently out-ranks human text, marginalizing human contributions in a "digital spiral of silence"; erroneous machine content crowds out accurate human knowledge and degrades the ecosystem over iterations.
  - *Limitations:* the abstract page does not report specific quantitative metrics for the simulation (verified this session), so the paper's force is directional rather than calibrated. It is a closed-loop simulation, not a measurement of the real web — a distinction routinely lost when it is cited.
  - *Related, at the agent rather than corpus level:* **Spiral of Silence in Large Language Model Agents** — Mingze Zhong et al. — arXiv:2510.02360 — Oct 2025 — **[listing-only]** — reportedly finds SoS-like conformity dynamics emerging in LLM collectives from history and persona signals alone. Relevant because multi-agent RAG systems can reproduce the dynamic internally, without any contaminated corpus at all.

- **Manipulating Large Language Models to Increase Product Visibility** — Aounon Kumar, Himabindu Lakkaraju — arXiv:2404.07981 — preprint (Apr 2024, rev. Sep 2024). A **strategic text sequence (STS)** appended to a product page substantially raises the chance the LLM ranks that product first. Demonstrated on a catalog of fictional coffee machines against both a rarely-recommended and a second-ranked product. Explicitly framed as the AI-era successor to SEO, with fair-competition consequences.
  - *Limitations:* fictional catalog, single narrow domain; STS strings are gradient-derived and typically unnatural, so perplexity filters are a plausible (if, per SCI-Defense 2026, insufficient) counter.

- **Ranking Manipulation for Conversational Search Engines** — Samuel Pfrommer, Yatong Bai, Tanmay Gautam, Somayeh Sojoudi — arXiv:2406.03589 — **EMNLP 2024 Main** (peer-reviewed). A tree-of-attacks jailbreaking technique "reliably promotes low-ranked products," demonstrated against **Perplexity.ai** on real consumer product websites. Finds different LLMs weight different factors — product name vs. document content vs. position in context — making the manipulation surface model-specific.

- **Adversarial Search Engine Optimization for Large Language Models** — Fredrik Nestaas, Edoardo Debenedetti, Florian Tramèr — arXiv:2406.18382 — preprint, cs.CR (Jun 2024). Introduces **Preference Manipulation Attacks**: attacker-controlled website content or plugin documentation that makes an LLM promote the attacker's products and discredit competitors. Demonstrated against **production Bing and Perplexity** and against plugin APIs for GPT-4 and Claude. The key structural result: attacks are individually rational but "the collective effect degrades the LLM's outputs for everyone" — an explicit **prisoner's dilemma** over shared retrieval infrastructure.
  - *Why this matters most:* this is the cleanest argument that GEO is not a marketing discipline but a tragedy-of-the-commons dynamic on the corpus a RAG system depends on.

- **Cocktail: A Comprehensive Information Retrieval Benchmark with LLM-Generated Documents Integration** — Sunhao Dai, Weihao Liu, Yuqi Zhou, Liang Pang, Rongju Ruan, Gang Wang, Zhenhua Dong, Jun Xu, Ji-Rong Wen — arXiv:2405.16546 — **Findings of ACL 2024** (peer-reviewed). 16 datasets pairing human-written and LLM-generated corpora across tasks and domains. Central finding: **a clear trade-off between ranking performance and source bias** in neural retrievers — you do not get both for free. Includes **NQ-UTD**, an up-to-date set built from recent-event queries to control for the LLM having memorized the evaluation corpus.
  - *Significance for a framework:* NQ-UTD is the design pattern any contamination-aware benchmark must copy — contamination evaluation is worthless if the generator has seen the gold data.

- **Exploring the Escalation of Source Bias in User, Data, and Recommender System Feedback Loop** — Yuqi Zhou, Sunhao Dai, Liang Pang, Gang Wang, Zhenhua Dong, Jun Xu, Ji-Rong Wen — arXiv:2405.17998 — preprint (May 2024, rev. Jun 2025). Short-term: AIGC ranks higher. Long-term, with realistic user simulators closing the loop: the preference **intensifies cyclically** as users engage with promoted AI content and the model retrains on that biased interaction data. Two harms separated cleanly — immediate unfair traffic distribution, and eventual quality degradation once AIGC dominates. Proposes an L1-loss debiasing objective.

- **Consent in Crisis: The Rapid Decline of the AI Data Commons** — Shayne Longpre, Robert Mahari, Ariel Lee + ~46 co-authors — arXiv:2407.14933 — preprint (Jul 2024). Longitudinal audit of 14,000 web domains. Verified numbers: **>5% of all C4 tokens fully restricted**; **>28% of the most actively maintained, critical C4 sources fully restricted**; **45% of C4 restricted under Terms of Service** crawling limits — a "rapid crescendo" within 2023–2024. Documents systematic inconsistency between ToS and robots.txt.
  - *Scope caveat that is constantly elided:* this measures **training-data** consent on C4, not retrieval-time access. It is evidence about corpus availability trends, not about what a live retriever can fetch today.

- **PoisonedRAG: Knowledge Corruption Attacks to Retrieval-Augmented Generation of Large Language Models** — Wei Zou, Runpeng Geng, Binghui Wang, Jinyuan Jia — arXiv:2402.07867 — **USENIX Security 2025** (peer-reviewed). The reference RAG poisoning result: **~90% attack success rate injecting five malicious texts per target question into a knowledge database of millions of texts**, in both black-box and white-box settings. Existing defenses evaluated and found insufficient.

### 2025 — mechanism work, and the ecosystem starts being measured

- **Perplexity Trap: PLM-Based Retrievers Overrate Low Perplexity Documents** — Haoyu Wang, Sunhao Dai, Haiyuan Zhao, Liang Pang, Xiao Zhang, Gang Wang, Zhenhua Dong, Jun Xu, Ji-Rong Wen — arXiv:2503.08684 — **ICLR 2025** (peer-reviewed). The first causal account: retrievers learn **perplexity itself as a relevance signal**, because the gradients of the language-modeling and retrieval objectives align positively. Low-perplexity documents — which is what LLM output is — get inflated relevance. Proposes **CDC (Causal Diagnosis and Correction)**, an *inference-time* debiasing method that estimates the perplexity bias effect and subtracts it from the relevance score; effective across three domains.
  - *Why this is the most actionable mitigation in the literature:* it requires no retraining and no AIGC detector, only a perplexity estimate — but see the direct contradiction from Xion & Nejdl (2026) below.

- **How Do LLM-Generated Texts Impact Term-Based Retrieval Models?** — Wei Huang, Keping Bi, Yinqiong Cai, Wei Chen, Jiafeng Guo, Xueqi Cheng — arXiv:2508.17715 — preprint (Aug 2025). Characterizes LLM text distributionally: "smoother high-frequency and steeper low-frequency Zipf slopes, higher term specificity, and greater document-level diversity." Central negative result: **BM25-class models show no inherent source preference** — they prioritize documents whose term distributions match the query's. Any apparent bias is query-distribution matching, not origin preference.
  - *Framework implication:* lexical retrieval is a partial *structural* control for source bias, not merely a recall complement. This is a load-bearing point for hybrid retrieval.

- **Is Misinformation More Open? A Study of robots.txt Gatekeeping on the Web** — Nicolas Steinacker-Olsztyn, Devashish Gosain, Ha Dao — arXiv:2510.10315 — **WWW 2026** (peer-reviewed). The single most important ecosystem measurement for this dimension. **60% of reputable news sites disallow at least one AI crawler, blocking on average 15.5 AI user agents; only 9.1% of misinformation sites do, blocking fewer than one on average.** Reputable-site AI-blocking rose **from 23% (Sep 2023) to nearly 60% (May 2025)**. Reputable sites also comply with their own declared directives more consistently than misinformation sites do.
  - *This is the retrieval-access asymmetry, quantified:* the web is being actively partitioned such that high-quality sources are opting out of AI access while low-quality sources remain wide open. Every "just retrieve from the live web" architecture inherits this skew.

- **Scrapers selectively respect robots.txt directives: evidence from a large-scale empirical study** — Taein Kim et al. — arXiv:2505.21733 — **[listing-only]** — reportedly finds AI crawlers rarely check robots.txt at all relative to traditional bots.
- **Somesite I Used To Crawl: Awareness, Agency and Efficacy in Protecting Content Creators From AI Crawlers** — Enze Liu et al. — arXiv:2411.15091 — **[listing-only]** — user study of content creators; reportedly finds awareness and efficacy gaps in blocking tools.
- **The Liabilities of Robots.txt** — Chien-yi Chang, Xin He — arXiv:2503.06035 — **[listing-only]** — contract/tort framing of robots.txt violations.
- **ai.txt: A Domain-Specific Language for Guiding AI Interactions with the Internet** — Yuekang Li et al. — arXiv:2505.07834 — **[listing-only]** — proposed successor to robots.txt with element-level and natural-language directives.

- **Machines in the Crowd? Measuring the Footprint of Machine-Generated Text on Reddit** — Lucio La Cava, Luca Maria Aiello, Andrea Tagarelli — arXiv:2510.07226 — preprint (Oct 2025). Two years of activity (2022–2024) across 51 subreddits with a state-of-the-art statistical MGT detector. Findings: synthetic text is **marginally present overall but peaks at up to 9% in some communities in some months**; concentrated in technical-knowledge and social-support subreddits and in a small fraction of users; **engagement comparable to, occasionally exceeding, human-authored content.** Authors describe their estimates as conservative.
  - *The single best available reality check on "the internet is mostly AI now."* On one heavily-discussed, heavily-licensed platform, over that window, it was single-digit percentages at the peak. Any framework motivation that asserts majority-AI corpora today is not supported by the measurement literature found this session.

- **Strategic Response of News Publishers to Generative AI** — Hangcheng Zhao, Ron Berman — arXiv:2512.24968 — preprint, econ.GN (Dec 2025, rev. Apr 2026). Difference-in-differences: **large publishers who block GenAI bots experience *reduced* website traffic relative to not blocking.** Publishers also shifted toward "richer content that is harder for LLMs to replicate, without increasing text volume," and increased editorial/content-production hiring.
  - *The counterintuitive finding that reframes the licensing wars:* blocking is costly to the blocker. This predicts partial, negotiated, tiered access rather than a clean walled-garden equilibrium.

- **Cloudflare, "Introducing pay per crawl"** — cloudflare.com blog, **1 Jul 2025** — **vendor**. Private-beta mechanism letting publishers charge AI crawlers per request via HTTP **402** and Web Bot Auth; three publisher options (allow free / charge / block). Framed from "hundreds of conversations with news organizations, publishers, and large-scale social media platforms."
  - *Verified absence:* this post contains **no** default-blocking policy statement and **no** crawl-to-referral ratio statistics. The widely-circulated crawl-to-visit ratio figures could not be sourced this session (the `content-independence-day` post returned HTTP 404 on fetch). **Do not cite those ratios from this file.**

### 2026 — the mechanism dispute, retrieval collapse, and the GEO industry

- **Retrieval Collapses When AI Pollutes the Web** — Hongyeon Yu, Dongchan Kim, Young-Bum Kim — arXiv:2602.16136 — **WWW 2026** (peer-reviewed) — Feb 2026. Models a two-stage failure: "(1) AI-generated content dominates search results, eroding source diversity, and (2) low-quality or adversarial content infiltrates the retrieval pipeline." Verified numbers: in the SEO-style-content scenario, **67% pool contamination produced over 80% exposure contamination** — i.e. contamination is *amplified* between the candidate pool and what actually reaches the model. In the adversarial scenario, **BM25 exposed ~19% of harmful content while LLM-based rankers suppressed more strongly.** The critical framing: pipelines "quietly shift toward synthetic evidence" while **accuracy metrics stay acceptable and mask the shift**.
  - *This is the most directly design-relevant result in the file.* It says (a) retrieval amplifies rather than dilutes contamination, (b) end-task accuracy is not a contamination detector, and (c) the ranker family determines the exposure profile — with lexical and neural rankers failing in *opposite* directions (BM25 lets adversarial content through; neural rankers amplify AIGC).

- **Data, Not Model: Explaining Bias toward LLM Texts in Neural Retrievers** — Wei Huang, Keping Bi, Yinqiong Cai, Wei Chen, Jiafeng Guo, Xueqi Cheng — arXiv:2604.06163 — preprint (Apr 2026). Argues source bias "stems from supervision in retrieval datasets rather than the models themselves." Non-semantic artifacts (fluency, term specificity) in training data mirror the human/LLM difference; in embedding space the bias direction runs from human toward LLM text; contrastive learning "inevitably absorbs the artifact imbalances." Two mitigations: **data-centric** (reduce artifact differences when constructing training data) and **vector-based** (project LLM-text embeddings off the identified bias direction).

- **Training-Induced Bias Toward LLM-Generated Content in Dense Retrieval** — William Xion, Wolfgang Nejdl — arXiv:2602.10833 — preprint (Feb 2026). Parallel human/LLM versions of SciFact, NQ320K, MS MARCO. **Unsupervised retrievers show inconsistent, dataset-dependent preferences; MS MARCO fine-tuning consistently shifts rankings toward LLM content; training on LLM-generated corpora induces pronounced pro-LLM preference.** Conclusion: "source bias is a training-induced phenomenon rather than an inherent property of dense retrievers." Critically, **the perplexity hypothesis "showed weak explanatory power" in their controlled evaluation** — a direct empirical challenge to Perplexity Trap.

- **Optimizing Visibility in Generative Engines: A Critical Survey of Generative Engine Optimization (2023–2026)** — Olivier Martinez — arXiv:2607.14035 — preprint (15 Jul 2026). Surveys **45 studies** from Nov 2023 to Jul 2026. Reframes GEO as "not a single ranking task but a stochastic, partially observable pipeline" from search activation through user behavior. Findings: topical relevance and context position are "the most reproducible levers"; generic approaches "transfer poorly"; "competition can erode individual gains"; citation-focused rewrites can **impair retrieval**. Commercial audits show "low source overlap, substantial run-to-run variability, and persistent fidelity gaps." The headline verdict: **"No reviewed technique shows a stable, longitudinal, cross-platform causal effect on organic discoverability or downstream behavior"** — only already-retrieved content shows measurable citation effects.

- **What Gets Cited: Competitive GEO in AI Answer Engines** — Rahul Vishwakarma, Shushant Kumar, Ratnesh Jamidar — arXiv:2605.25517 — preprint (25 May 2026). A controlled **two-document RAG testbed**: exactly two candidates compete for citation, **252,000 trials across six LLMs, 18 content factors varied**, with order held fixed and sources anonymized to kill position bias. Strongest effects: **topical relevance and list position**. Consistently positive: **explicit price information and recent timestamps**. Modest: completeness and trust signals. Minimal: **formatting changes alone**. Ships a reproducible protocol and a practitioner checklist, pilot-tested at Sprinklr.
  - *Note the uncomfortable finding for trust-labeling designs:* "trust signals" moved citation only modestly, while **recency** moved it consistently. An answer engine that rewards freshness over provenance is exactly the engine AIGC farms are optimized for.

- **SafeGEO: Understanding Generative Engine Optimization Risks in Recommendation Agents** — Qianfeng Wen, Yifan Simon Liu, Xin Liu, Difan Jiao, Blair Yang, Junda Wu, Zhenwei Tang — arXiv:2606.28356 — preprint (Jun 2026). Evaluation suite: **22 attack variants across 600 recommendation scenarios**. GEO attacks **increase the rate at which flawed products enter the recommendation set by up to 83.2%**. Simple defenses (defensive prompting, structured evidence checks) reduce harmful promotion **by up to 39.2%** — meaningful but insufficient; "GEO remains a serious risk despite developer-side mitigation."

- **GEO-Bench: Benchmarking Ranking Manipulation in Generative Engine Optimization** — Ojas Nimase, Zhe Chen, Gengpei Qi, Yue Zhao, Xiyang Hu — arXiv:2605.29107 — preprint (May 2026). Unifies black-box prompt attacks (TAP, Zero-Shot), white-box gradient attacks (STS, RAF, StealthRank) and **ten white-hat C-SEO defenses** under one protocol on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct across five datasets. Metrics split effectiveness (NRG, Success@α, Promote@α) from **stealth** (keyword violation rate, perplexity ratio). Key results: effectiveness and stealth trade off; **black-box content rewriting matches or exceeds gradient methods for rank promotion while producing more fluent text** and evading keyword- and perplexity-based detection on some domains; **access level does not predict attack strength**.
  - *The most consequential single sentence for defense design:* the fluent, natural, black-box rewrite is now as strong as the gradient-optimized gibberish string. Perplexity filters — the cheap defense everyone reaches for, and the same signal CDC uses for debiasing — are therefore losing their grip.

- **SCI-Defense: Defending Manipulation Attacks from Generative Engine Optimization** — Xucheng Yu, Haibo Jin, Huimin Zeng, Haohan Wang — arXiv:2605.21948 — preprint (May 2026). Three components: perplexity detection, **Semantic Integrity Scoring** over four manipulation dimensions (Authority Attribution, Narrative Purposiveness, Comparative Claims, Temporal Claims), and Inter-Candidate Detection comparing patterns across candidates. On 600 Amazon product descriptions across six categories: **precision 1.000 with zero false positives; recall 1.000 (String attacks), 0.952 (Reasoning), 0.830 (Review)**. Reports that **existing defenses — PPL-only filters, safety classifiers, and paraphrasing — achieve zero recall** against semantic manipulation.
  - *Critique:* perfect precision on a 600-item single-domain product corpus against a known attack set is a weak generalization signal; the four SIS dimensions are hand-specified and therefore attackable by construction.

- **Position: Generative Engine Optimization Creates Underexamined Risks, Governance Must Target Concentration, Disclosure, and Academic Blind Spots** — Yizhu Wen, Nan Zhang, Haohan Yuan, Xun Chen, Haopeng Zhang, Hanqing Guo — arXiv:2606.12439 — preprint (May 2026). Three risks: (1) **concentrated influence** from low contestability and high system sensitivity; (2) **undisclosed commercial influence embedded in the evidence and the reasoning**, invisible in the generated answer; (3) **academic–industry blind spots** from visibility and evaluation asymmetries between offline setups and deployed systems. Recommends answer-level governance: contestability mechanisms, high-precision disclosure, **black-box auditing of material influence**, and deployment-aligned metrics tracking exposure persistence.

- **How Generative AI Disrupts Search: An Empirical Study of Google Search, Gemini, and AI Overviews** — Riley Grossman, Songjiang Liu, Michael K. Chen, Mike Smith, Cristian Borcea, Yi Chen — arXiv:2604.27790 — **accepted SIGIR 2026** (peer-reviewed) — Apr 2026. **11,500 real user queries** across Google Search, AI Overviews and Gemini Flash 2.5. **AIOs appear for 51.5% of representative real-user queries.** Source overlap across platforms is **under 0.2 average Jaccard similarity**. Traditional search favors popular/institutional (.gov/.edu) sources; generative systems are "significantly more likely to retrieve Google-owned content." **Sites blocking Google's AI crawler are significantly less likely to be retrieved by AIOs, despite the system having access to the content.** AIOs are also less consistent across two runs of the same query and less robust to minor query edits.
  - *Three separate framework-relevant findings in one paper:* platform source distributions barely overlap (so "what the web says" is engine-specific); crawler consent has direct retrieval-visibility consequences beyond training; and answer-engine retrieval is non-deterministic run-to-run.

- **When Poison Fails After Retrieval: Revisiting Corpus Poisoning under Chunking and Reranking Pipelines** — Xi Nie et al. — arXiv:2606.11265 — **[listing-only]** — reportedly shows existing poisoning attacks degrade under realistic multi-stage retrieval, and proposes chunk-aware, rerank-consistent poisoning. The most important listing-only item here: it implies published attack success rates are **upper bounds measured against simplified pipelines**.
- Further 2025–26 poisoning attack/defense work, all **[listing-only]**: Joint-GCG (arXiv:2506.06151, Haowei Wang), MIRAGE (arXiv:2512.08289, Tailun Chen), unsupervised continuous-space poisoning (arXiv:2504.17884, Yongkang Li), DIGA influential-token attack (arXiv:2503.21315, Cheng Wang), TrustRAG (arXiv:2501.00879, Huichi Zhou), EcoSafeRAG (arXiv:2505.13506, Ruobing Yao), SeCon-RAG (arXiv:2510.09710, Xiaonan Si), RAGPart/RAGMask (arXiv:2512.24268, Pankayaraj Pathmanathan), ProGRank (arXiv:2603.22934, Xiangyu Yin), Semantic Chameleon (arXiv:2603.18034, Scott Thornton), token-influence attribution for poisoned corpora (arXiv:2606.25721, Yan-Lun Chen), RAGuard (arXiv:2607.26339, Pushkal Kumar), DenialRAG (arXiv:2608.02678, Abay Zhurekbay), and robustness of LLM-based dense retrievers (arXiv:2604.16576, Yongkang Li).

## State of the art (mid-2026)

**What is solidly established.**

1. **Source bias is real and reproducible** across dense retrievers and rerankers, across text and text-image retrieval, on controlled parallel human/LLM corpora (Dai KDD'24; Xu SIGIR'24; Cocktail ACL Findings'24; Xion & Nejdl 2026).
2. **Lexical retrieval does not share it.** BM25-class models match query term distributions and show no origin preference (Huang 2508.17715). This makes hybrid retrieval a *structural* mitigation, not just a recall trick.
3. **Contamination amplifies through the pipeline, and accuracy hides it.** 67% pool → >80% exposure, with acceptable end-task metrics throughout (Yu WWW'26).
4. **Answer engines are manipulable, cheaply, in production.** Preference manipulation on live Bing/Perplexity (Nestaas 2024); tree-of-attacks promotion on Perplexity.ai (Pfrommer EMNLP'24); +83.2% flawed-product entry in recommendation agents (SafeGEO 2026); fluent black-box rewrites now match gradient attacks while evading perplexity detection (GEO-Bench 2026).
5. **The web is partitioning asymmetrically against quality.** 60% of reputable news sites block ≥1 AI crawler vs 9.1% of misinformation sites (WWW'26); blocking Google's AI crawler measurably reduces AIO retrieval (SIGIR'26); blocking costs large publishers traffic (Zhao & Berman 2026).
6. **Answer-engine source distributions barely overlap and are run-to-run unstable** (<0.2 Jaccard; reduced consistency across identical repeated queries — SIGIR'26).

**What is contested or unresolved.**

- **The mechanism of source bias.** Four incompatible accounts, detailed below.
- **Whether GEO works at all in the wild.** The field's only critical survey finds no technique with a stable, longitudinal, cross-platform causal effect on organic discoverability (Martinez 2026).
- **How much of the retrievable web is actually AI-generated.** The only prevalence measurement located this session (Reddit, 2022–2024) reports peaks of ~9% in some communities, not majority contamination.
- **Whether recursive contamination is self-limiting.** See §Collapse.

**What is weakest — and this is the important gap.** Detection and provenance. There is no verified, deployable way to label the origin of retrieved *text* at scale. This undercuts every mitigation that presupposes an "is this AIGC?" oracle.

## The mechanism dispute

The field agrees the effect exists and disagrees, four ways, about what causes it. This matters because **each account implies mitigation at a different layer**, and a framework that picks the wrong layer buys nothing.

| Account | Claim | Mitigation layer implied | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Semantic compression** | LLM text has more focused semantics, less noise → easier matching | Debiased training constraint | Dai et al., KDD 2024 (peer-reviewed) |
| **Perplexity as learned relevance** | LM and retrieval gradients align, so low perplexity is read as relevant | **Inference-time** score correction (CDC) | Wang et al., ICLR 2025 (peer-reviewed) |
| **Training-data artifacts** | Fluency/term-specificity imbalances in supervision are absorbed by contrastive learning; a bias *direction* exists in embedding space | Training-data construction, or embedding projection | Huang et al., arXiv:2604.06163 (preprint) |
| **Training-induced, not perplexity** | Unsupervised retrievers are inconsistent; MS MARCO fine-tuning creates the bias; **perplexity has weak explanatory power** | Fine-tuning data choice | Xion & Nejdl, arXiv:2602.10833 (preprint) |
| **No bias in lexical models** | BM25 matches query term distributions; apparent bias is distributional, not origin-based | Use lexical retrieval as a control | Huang et al., arXiv:2508.17715 (preprint) |

Three things follow. First, **the peer-reviewed accounts (compression, perplexity) are both challenged by 2026 preprints**, so the mitigation with the best deployment story (CDC, inference-time, no retraining) rests on a mechanism another group failed to replicate as explanatory. Second, if Xion & Nejdl and Huang et al. are right, source bias is a **data-curation and fine-tuning problem**, which is good news — it is fixable without architectural change — but it also means the bias is **retriever-specific and non-transferable**, so no framework can assume a fixed correction. Third, GEO-Bench's finding that fluent black-box rewrites evade perplexity detection means **the perplexity signal is degrading as an instrument at the same time it is being contested as a mechanism.** Any design that leans on perplexity should treat it as a decaying asset.

## Detection, watermarking & provenance — the weak leg

**Detection is not reliable enough to gate a corpus on.** All of the following are preprints; all were surfaced by arXiv API listing, and the two most damaging are marked accordingly:

- **Spotlights and Blindspots: Evaluating Machine-Generated Text Detection** — Kevin Stowe, Kailash Patil — arXiv:2604.16607 — **[listing-only]** — reportedly evaluates a set of detection models and finds inconsistent cross-dataset performance, poor results on novel human text, and heavy sensitivity to methodological choices.
- **Identifying Bias in Machine-generated Text Detection** — Kevin Stowe et al. — arXiv:2512.09292 — **[listing-only]** — reportedly finds ELL (English-language-learner) essays, and non-White ELL essays especially, disproportionately misclassified as machine-generated. **This is a fairness landmine for any framework that down-weights retrieved documents by detector score.**
- **Paraphrasing Attack Resilience of Various AI-Generated Text Detection Methods** — Andrii Shportko, Inessa Verbitsky — arXiv:2605.14240 — **[listing-only]** — reportedly a "dichotomy of performance versus resilience," with the strongest detectors losing the most under paraphrase.
- **When Personalization Tricks Detectors: The Feature-Inversion Trap** — Lang Gao et al. — arXiv:2510.12476 — **[listing-only]**; **Machine Text Detectors are Membership Inference Attacks** — Ryuto Koike et al. — arXiv:2510.19492 — **[listing-only]** (detection and MIA exploit the same distributional signal — with the privacy implications that entails). Newer robustness proposals: WaveDetect (arXiv:2606.23336), T-Detect (arXiv:2507.23577), REACT adversarial training (arXiv:2605.02374), DMAP (arXiv:2602.11871), Luminol-AIDetect (arXiv:2604.25860) — all **[listing-only]**.

**The RAG watermarking literature solves a different problem.** An arXiv query for `watermark AND retrieval-augmented` returns, essentially without exception, **corpus intellectual-property protection**: RAG-WM (arXiv:2501.05249), watermarked canary documents for membership inference (arXiv:2502.10673), CoT-embedded knowledge-base watermarks (arXiv:2502.10440), AQUA for multimodal knowledge copyright (arXiv:2506.10030), "Who Stole Your Data?" dual-layer RAG plagiarism detection (arXiv:2510.07728), ImageSentinel (arXiv:2510.12119) — all **[listing-only]**, all preprints. These answer *"did someone copy my knowledge base?"*, not *"was this retrieved passage machine-written?"* **Do not conflate them.** Notably, arXiv:2601.00274 ("Making Theft Useless," **[listing-only]**) reportedly argues watermarking is ineffective for private-use theft — i.e. the technique is contested even for its actual purpose.

**Provenance in RAG exists as engineering, not as origin-labeling.** The `provenance AND RAG` query returns ~20 preprints (Jun–Aug 2026) in which "provenance" almost always means **internal lineage** — which chunk, which document, which version, hash-chained — rather than **external origin** (human vs. machine, publisher identity, first-publication date). Representative, all **[listing-only]** preprints: ContextNest verifiable context governance with SHA-256 hash-chained version histories (arXiv:2607.02116), SMSR HMAC-signed memory writes (arXiv:2606.12703), TriShieldRAG provenance-and-consistency-weighted trust scoring against knowledge corruption (arXiv:2607.23838), a provenance-based instruction hierarchy against prompt injection (arXiv:2606.19660), Chronofy temporal-decay validity (arXiv:2607.20560), DocTrace node-level evidence provenance (arXiv:2608.03292), and hubness control with provenance as a complement to ingestion gating (arXiv:2606.19692).

The one fetched item in this space is instructive precisely because it is so simple:

- **Source-Aware Reranking for Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Reliability Prior Approach** — Yuktha Tata Koganti, Hugo Garrido-Lestache Belinchon — arXiv:2607.22584 — preprint. Multiplies similarity by a source-type credibility weight: `score(q,d) = sim(q,d) × λ(s)`. On a **120-document health corpus**, Precision@5 rose **0.48 → 0.72** and adversarially-placed low-credibility documents were retrieved less often. [uncertain] The abstract page reports a June 8, 2026 submission date while the arXiv ID is in the 2607 block; treat the date as approximate.
  - *Critique:* 120 documents, one domain, hand-assigned per-source-type weights. It is a proof of concept, not evidence. But it is the clearest existing statement of the design pattern — **credibility as a multiplicative prior on the retrieval score** — and its own authors note applicability depends on reliable source metadata, which is exactly what does not exist.

**C2PA / content credentials: declared gap.** The C2PA specification index (spec.c2pa.org, versions 1.0–2.4 listed) was fetched this session and contains **no technical content** on manifests, hard bindings, soft bindings or assertions; the index page makes **no mention of text assets**. Rather than describe the standard from memory: *content-provenance standards in the C2PA family are image/video/audio-centric, and their applicability to plain-text corpus items was not verified this session.* The general obstacle is structural and worth stating without needing the spec: provenance schemes of this kind generally bind to a *whole asset*, whereas a RAG corpus item is a *chunk* extracted from a re-encoded, re-hosted, often re-written derivative — on any such scheme the binding is destroyed by the ingestion pipeline itself.

## Collapse discourse — and the case against over-claiming it

- **The Curse of Recursion: Training on Generated Data Makes Models Forget** — Ilia Shumailov, Zakhar Shumaylov, Yiren Zhao, Yarin Gal, Nicolas Papernot, Ross Anderson — arXiv:2305.17493 — preprint (May 2023, rev. Apr 2024). Defines **model collapse**: training on model-generated content causes "irreversible defects... where tails of the original content distribution disappear," demonstrated for VAEs, GMMs and LLMs. Concludes genuine human data rises in value. *[uncertain] A later, differently-titled Nature paper by substantially the same group is widely cited alongside this; that article was behind an identity-provider redirect and was not fetched, so it is not cited here and should not be merged with this entry.*
- **AI and the Problem of Knowledge Collapse** — Andrew J. Peterson — arXiv:2404.03502 — **AI and Society**, published 19 Jan 2025 (peer-reviewed). Models communities choosing between traditional inquiry and discounted AI assistance. LLMs generate toward the center of the distribution; cheap AI access narrows exposure to tail knowledge. Headline: **a 20% discount on AI-generated content produces public beliefs 2.3× further from the truth than with no discount.** Peterson explicitly argues the outcome is *not* inevitable — unlike models, humans can strategically seek diverse sources.
- **The counter-evidence wave, all [listing-only] preprints:** KITE — learning from synthetic data without model collapse in iterative instruction tuning (arXiv:2607.17043, Xiaonan Luo); recursively trained diffusion models with an annealed-truncation schedule that reportedly eliminates compounding (arXiv:2606.13796, Naïl B. Khelifa); collapse precipitated specifically by **sample-selection bias** under low-resource verification (arXiv:2606.13732, Xinbao Qiao); human curation backfiring in multi-model loops (arXiv:2605.29267, Yang Zhang); and a critical treatment of the knowledge-collapse model emphasizing externalities (arXiv:2607.13272, Maher Kallel).

**The honest position for a framework paper:** collapse results are largely about *recursive training* under *replacement* of real data, with a live and growing literature arguing the dynamics are controllable via curation, truncation and accumulation. They are **not** a demonstration that a retrieval corpus degrades irreversibly. The defensible motivation is narrower and better supported: **retrieval-side amplification of synthetic content, invisible to accuracy metrics** (Yu WWW'26), plus **origin-skewed access** (Steinacker-Olsztyn WWW'26). Motivate on those; cite collapse as context, not as proof.

## Failure modes & critiques

**F1. The field cannot agree what causes source bias, so mitigations target different objects.** Compression (Dai) vs. learned perplexity (Wang, ICLR'25) vs. training-data artifacts (Huang 2604.06163) vs. fine-tuning-induced with perplexity explicitly weak (Xion & Nejdl 2602.10833) vs. absent in lexical models (Huang 2508.17715). A framework that ships one correction is betting on one mechanism.

**F2. Contamination is amplified by the pipeline and invisible to standard evaluation.** 67% → >80% pool-to-exposure amplification with accuracy metrics still acceptable (Yu, WWW'26). **Any RAG evaluation harness that measures only answer quality is blind to this failure by construction** — this is the strongest single indictment of current RAG evaluation practice in this dimension.

**F3. Lexical and neural rankers fail in opposite directions.** BM25 exposed ~19% of adversarial content while LLM rankers suppressed it better (Yu, WWW'26); conversely BM25 shows no AIGC preference while neural retrievers do (Huang 2508.17715). **Hybrid retrieval does not average the failures away — it inherits both**, unless fusion is explicitly designed around which failure each arm contributes.

**F4. Perplexity is failing simultaneously as a debiasing signal and as a defense.** CDC (ICLR'25) corrects *using* perplexity; SCI-Defense reports PPL-only filters at **zero recall** against semantic manipulation; GEO-Bench finds fluent black-box rewrites evading perplexity detection while matching gradient attacks. The signal is being contested and evaded at once.

**F5. Detection-based mitigation imports a fairness hazard.** Reported disproportionate misclassification of ELL and non-White ELL text as machine-generated (arXiv:2512.09292, **[listing-only]**), plus paraphrase fragility and personalization inversion. Down-weighting retrieved documents by detector score means systematically down-weighting non-native-English authors. This is a reason to prefer *soft, auditable* origin scores over hard filters — and to log the score's influence.

**F6. The watermarking/provenance literature does not address the contamination problem.** RAG watermarking is corpus-IP protection; RAG "provenance" is internal lineage. Neither yields origin labels. Cryptographic provenance is defeated by chunking and re-hosting before it reaches the index.

**F7. GEO's efficacy is a null result dressed as an industry.** At least 23 GEO papers in one truncated eight-month listing, the large majority proposing methods rather than measuring effects (peer-review status unverified — see method note); the one critical survey finds **no** technique with a stable, longitudinal, cross-platform causal effect on organic discoverability, and notes citation-focused rewrites can *impair* retrieval. Meanwhile the *attack* literature — which measures manipulation of a ranking, not organic discovery — is far more convincing (Nestaas; Pfrommer EMNLP'24; SafeGEO). **The distinction to hold: manipulating a fixed candidate set is demonstrated; achieving organic visibility is not.** Vendor GEO marketing conflates them.

**F8. Optimization is individually rational and collectively destructive.** Nestaas et al.'s prisoner's dilemma: each publisher gains by optimizing; the shared corpus degrades for everyone. No participant has a unilateral incentive to stop, so this does not self-correct — it must be handled at the retrieval layer.

**F9. Access asymmetry actively selects for low-quality sources.** 60% of reputable news sites block ≥1 AI crawler vs 9.1% of misinformation sites, with reputable blocking rising 23%→60% in 20 months (WWW'26); AI-crawler-blocking sites are significantly less likely to be retrieved by AI Overviews (SIGIR'26). **Open-web retrieval is becoming a biased sample in the precise direction that matters**, and the bias is a consequence of consent, not of ranking.

**F10. The corpus is engine-specific and non-reproducible.** <0.2 Jaccard source overlap across Google Search / AIO / Gemini, with run-to-run instability on identical queries (SIGIR'26). Any system built on a hosted answer/search API has a corpus it cannot characterize, reproduce, or audit.

**F11. Blocking is costly to blockers, so clean provenance regimes will not emerge.** Large publishers who block GenAI bots lose traffic (Zhao & Berman 2026). Expect negotiated, tiered, partial access — permanent heterogeneity in what a retriever may see, differing per operator.

**F12. Published poisoning success rates are probably upper bounds.** arXiv:2606.11265 (**[listing-only]**) reportedly shows existing attacks degrade under realistic chunking and reranking. PoisonedRAG's 90%-at-five-documents should be read as a simplified-pipeline result — which cuts both ways: it means defenses partly exist by accident, and it means attack papers are not measuring production systems.

**F13. Prevalence is over-claimed in both directions.** "The internet is mostly AI slop" is not supported by the only prevalence measurement found here (Reddit peaks ~9% in some communities, 2022–2024, conservative estimates). But the reverse extrapolation is equally unsupported: Yu et al. measured amplification only in a **high-contamination regime** (67% pool → >80% exposure, roughly 1.2×), which says nothing about the amplification factor at single-digit pool prevalence, and the relationship is not known to be monotone. **The low-prevalence regime is simply unmeasured.** Prevalence and exposure must be measured separately — arguments that use one to reason about the other are unsupported regardless of direction, including the ones in this file's own favour.

**F14. Freshness rewards contamination.** In the 252,000-trial citation study, recent timestamps consistently increased citation while trust signals helped only modestly. Generated content is trivially cheap to make recent; authoritative human content is not. **A freshness-weighted retriever is a contamination-weighted retriever.**

## Relevance to a next-generation agentic RAG framework

Concretely, and traceable to the evidence above:

**1. Make origin a first-class, *soft*, multi-signal index field — never a hard filter.** Every corpus item carries a structured origin record: publisher/domain identity, first-observed timestamp, crawl path, whether the item is a derivative of another indexed item, and a *calibrated, versioned* AIGC likelihood. Soft because detection is unreliable and demonstrably biased against non-native English authors (F5); multi-signal because no single detector survives paraphrase (F5); versioned because the detector will be replaced and every historical score must remain interpretable. Store the score's *contribution* to each ranking decision so it can be audited and retracted — this is what the GEO governance position paper means by "black-box auditing of material influence."

**2. Adopt credibility as a multiplicative prior on retrieval score, with per-source weights that are data, not code.** `score(q,d) = sim(q,d) × λ(source)` is the pattern (arXiv:2607.22584), and its 0.48→0.72 Precision@5 on 120 health documents is a hint, not a result. The framework contribution is making λ a first-class, inspectable, per-tenant, per-domain configuration with provenance on the weights themselves — because "reliable source metadata" is exactly what the literature says is missing.

**3. Add inference-time source-bias correction as an optional, *evaluated* reranker stage.** CDC (ICLR'25) is the only mitigation that needs no retraining. But given F1 and F4, ship it as a measured stage with a bias metric attached, not as an always-on default — and measure it per retriever, since Xion & Nejdl show the bias is fine-tuning-specific and does not transfer.

**4. Enforce origin-diversity constraints at *exposure* time, not pool time.** This is the direct design consequence of 67%→>80% amplification. The retrieved set handed to the model should satisfy explicit composition constraints — maximum share from any single origin class, minimum share of independently-originated evidence, no more than *k* items that are derivatives of the same upstream document. Amplification happens in ranking, so the constraint must bind on the final set.

**5. Instrument corpus composition as a first-class metric, decoupled from answer quality.** "Quality metrics mask the shift" (Yu, WWW'26) is a direct instruction: track AIGC share of pool, AIGC share of exposure, origin-diversity of exposed sets, and derivative-chain depth, and alarm on *drift in those* independently of task accuracy. A framework whose only dashboard is answer quality cannot see its own contamination.

**6. Design fusion around the *opposite* failure modes of the two retrieval arms.** BM25 is a control for AIGC preference but leaks adversarial content; neural ranking suppresses adversarial content but amplifies AIGC. Fusion weights should be a function of the *threat* the deployment faces, and the framework should expose that as a policy choice rather than a tuned constant. The corollary is a positive one: keeping a lexical arm is a cheap structural mitigation, not legacy baggage.

**7. Decouple freshness from authority in the ranking objective.** F14 says a naive recency boost is a contamination boost. Recency should be gated on origin class — recency from a high-λ source is a strong signal; recency from an unknown or high-AIGC-likelihood source is close to no signal at all.

**8. Treat GEO-poisoning suites as a standing regression gate, not a one-off audit.** GEO-Bench's protocol (effectiveness *and* stealth metrics, black-box and white-box attacks, white-hat defenses in the same harness), SafeGEO's 22 variants × 600 scenarios, and the GPE/ToE fact-verification-under-poisoning suites are the shape of the gate. Every ranker or fusion change should re-run it. GEO-Bench's finding that access level does not predict attack strength means you cannot skip black-box attacks because you assume weights are private.

**9. Do not assume open-web parity across operators.** Crawler-consent asymmetry (F9), blocker-cost dynamics (F11) and engine-specific non-overlapping source distributions (F10) mean the accessible corpus differs per operator, per licensing posture, and per run. The framework should make *corpus reachability* explicit — declared allowlists, licensed feeds, consent state per source, and a recorded snapshot identity — so retrieval is reproducible and the sampling bias is legible rather than latent. Anything built on a hosted answer API should be labelled as having an uncharacterizable corpus.

**10. Assume derivative content and detect it structurally, not statistically.** The most tractable contamination signal available today is not "was this machine-written?" but "is this a near-duplicate or paraphrase of something already indexed, published later, from a lower-λ source?" Derivative-chain detection uses infrastructure a RAG system already has (embeddings, near-dup detection, timestamps) and sidesteps the detector-reliability and fairness problems entirely. This is, on the evidence surveyed here, the highest-value under-explored mitigation.

**11. Copy NQ-UTD's discipline into evaluation.** Contamination benchmarks must use post-cutoff queries and corpora (Cocktail's NQ-UTD), or the generator's memorization confounds every measurement.

## Open problems

1. **Settle the mechanism.** A controlled study that jointly manipulates perplexity, training-data artifact balance, and fine-tuning corpus composition, reconciling Wang (ICLR'25), Huang (2604.06163) and Xion & Nejdl (2602.10833). Until this is settled, mitigation choice is guesswork.
2. **Origin labeling for text at chunk granularity.** Cryptographic provenance dies at ingestion; statistical detection is unreliable and biased. Nothing in the surveyed literature closes this gap. Derivative-chain and first-publication-priority approaches are the most promising untried direction.
3. **Prevalence measurement for retrievable corpora.** The only prevalence study found is Reddit-only, 2022–2024, conservative. There is no measurement of AIGC share in *what retrievers actually return* on the open web. Pool prevalence and exposure prevalence must be measured as separate quantities.
4. **Exposure-contamination metrics with real ground truth.** Yu et al.'s amplification result comes from a simulation. No standard metric or benchmark exists for measuring exposure contamination in a live pipeline, which is precisely why F2 is invisible in practice.
5. **Defenses that survive fluent black-box rewrites.** GEO-Bench shows the fluent attack is now as strong as the gradient attack; SCI-Defense's near-perfect numbers are on 600 single-domain items against a known attack set with hand-specified manipulation dimensions. Generalizing defense to unseen semantic manipulation is open.
6. **Equilibrium analysis of the GEO commons.** Nestaas et al. name the prisoner's dilemma but do not solve it. What retrieval-side mechanism removes the unilateral incentive to optimize? Is there a ranking rule under which GEO investment has zero expected return — and is it compatible with serving useful fresh content at all?
7. **Fair contamination mitigation.** How do you down-weight synthetic content without systematically down-weighting non-native-English human authors? This needs to be a stated design constraint, not an afterthought, and no surveyed work treats it as one.
8. **Retrieval under partitioned access.** With reputable sources opting out and low-quality sources open, what does a *quality-corrected* retrieval distribution look like, and can a framework estimate and correct the selection bias it cannot observe?
