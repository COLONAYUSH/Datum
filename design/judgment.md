# Judgment — synthesis rationale for Datum

*Companion to `FRAMEWORK.md`. Documents the score arithmetic, the winner rationale, the full graft ledger (accepted and rejected, with reasoning), and the contradictions resolved between stances. Two of the five source proposals are both titled "Strata" in the source material (`proposal-systems-db.md` and `proposal-memory-os.md`); both are referred to by **stance name only** throughout this document — systems-db and memory-os — never by their shared title, to keep the score table and graft ledger legible.*

### 1.1 Where the lenses disagree with each other, and why that matters more than the winner margin

The inter-lens spread per stance (max score minus min score across the three lenses) is itself evidence, not noise — it tells you which grafts are robust across perspectives and which are lens-contingent:

| Stance | Spread (max − min) | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| memory-os | **6.5** (47.5 enterprise-operator − 41 staff-engineer) | Largest spread in the panel. The enterprise-operator lens rewards the concrete mechanisms (crypto-shred, namespace ACL, published low-privilege-recall metric) most highly of any stance-lens pairing; the staff-engineer lens scores it *lowest of all five stances on that lens*, specifically citing production-scale risk (KMS as a catastrophic single point of failure, combinatorial namespace explosion). |
| learned-policy | 6.0 (46 research-reviewer − 40 enterprise-operator) | The research-reviewer lens rewards the self-skeptical literature audit; the enterprise-operator lens penalizes it hardest of the five stances for compliance exposure (a self-modifying retrieval system is a harder validation-audit object than a frozen one). |
| systems-db | 5.5 (51.5 enterprise-operator − 46 staff-engineer) | Tight and directionally consistent — even the *lowest* of systems-db's three scores (46, staff-engineer) is still that lens's 2nd-highest score overall, beaten only by its own top pick (agent-native, 47). Strength is robust across lenses, not lens-contingent. |
| agent-native | 5.0 (47 staff-engineer − 42 enterprise-operator) | The engineering lens rewards the frozen-type-table discipline; the operator lens penalizes the self-reported, unaudited capability manifest as a governance gap. |
| evidence-first | **4.5** (47 enterprise-operator − 42.5 research-reviewer) | Smallest spread — all three lenses converge independently on both the same strongest mechanism (the out-of-band envelope) *and* the same fatal flaw (authority-tier non-generalization), in near-identical language. |

Two consequences follow directly from this table, and both are decisions this document already made — the spread is why those decisions were correct, not merely defensible. **memory-os's largest-spread mechanisms (crypto-shred, namespace ACL) are grafted with bounds attached, not accepted outright** — the graft ledger's own hedging (§3.3) is exactly proportional to how much the lenses disagree about the underlying mechanism's real-world risk. **evidence-first's smallest-spread mechanisms are grafted or rejected with the highest confidence in this document** — the out-of-band envelope (G4) is accepted unconditionally and the authority-tier axiom (R2) is rejected unconditionally, because unanimous agreement across three independent perspectives is the strongest evidentiary signal available in this synthesis, stronger than any single lens's score.

## 2. Winner rationale

### 2.1 The four-CONFIRMED mapping

`common-issues.md`'s adversarial verification confirmed exactly four of the taxonomy's top twelve issues at high confidence, after two independent skeptic rounds: **CI-03** (filter-channel-is-ACL-channel), **CI-05** (authorization as a paid tier), **CI-07** (ingest-time one-way doors), **CI-08** (churn/demotion/death). Every other top-12 issue was weakened and restated; CI-13 through CI-27 were never adversarially verified at all.

systems-db's own four founding axioms (logical/physical plan separation; materialized-view lineage; transactional mutation; non-optional principal/policy/budget) map **1:1** onto exactly these four confirmed issues — the research-reviewer lens states this explicitly as its own decisive reason: *"its four axioms map 1:1 onto the taxonomy's four CONFIRMED issues (CI-03/05/07/08)."* No other stance's axiom set lines up this cleanly against the best-evidenced subset of the taxonomy rather than the weakened-and-restated majority. Datum's own nine axioms (`FRAMEWORK.md` §Design axioms) preserve this mapping explicitly and state it up front, per the task's instruction that the Verification lines matter.

### 2.2 The correction that had to happen regardless of skeleton choice

All three lenses, independently, named the same fatal flaw in systems-db's own proposal: the cost-based Optimizer is admitted vaporware — *"a rule table wearing an optimizer's costume,"* a *"multi-year systems-research undertaking… unsolved even for filtered ANN alone"* (systems-db's own Feasibility section concedes this). research-reviewer's fatal-flaw line for systems-db is explicit that this is *"largely aspirational rather than demonstrated."* staff-engineer's fatal-flaw line is nearly identical. enterprise-operator's is sharper still: *"if that optimizer is read as the paper's central claim rather than as future work sitting atop an already-complete substrate, the design ships without validating its own thesis."*

This is not a defect to route around while still centering the optimizer — it is the specific reason a synthesis cannot simply adopt systems-db's proposal as written and call it done. **Datum's thesis is the logical/physical plan separation, not the planner that occupies one slot inside it.** Plan-selection is demoted to a versioned policy in a registry slot; v1 ships that slot filled with a declared, dated rule table (fully buildable today, zero research dependency); the promotion-gate machinery grafted from learned-policy (§3.4 below) is what lets that table improve safely on a deployment's own traffic instead of either freezing forever (CI-14) or waiting on unsolved cost-based-optimization research to mature before shipping anything. This single reframing is also how Datum honors the *dissenting* staff-engineer lens without switching skeletons: that lens's own stated reason for preferring agent-native was *"no unproven crown-jewel dependency"* — scored explicitly against systems-db for leaning on the optimizer. Removing the crown-jewel dependency from the winning skeleton answers the staff-engineer lens's objection directly, rather than out-voting it.

### 2.3 Why not the other four, as skeletons

- **agent-native** (2nd, 135.5) is the strongest runner-up and the *only* stance to win a lens outright besides systems-db. Its fatal flaw, per all three lenses, is that its enforcement mechanisms are weaker than they read: the capability manifest is self-reported and unaudited, and its own conformance suite "may end up catching only what Legend itself builds, not what it wraps" (research-reviewer, quoting the proposal's own Risks section). Its mechanisms survive richly in Datum as grafts (§3.1) precisely because the staff-engineer lens's endorsement of them was specific and mechanism-level, not skeleton-level.
- **evidence-first** (3rd, 133.5) has the single sharpest security mechanism in the batch (the out-of-band evidentiary envelope, independently graft-cited by all three lenses) but its central axiom — a registry-bound authority hierarchy — was named as the fatal flaw by all three lenses using nearly identical language: it "generalizes a single medical-vertical ablation… into a universal design commitment," and "most enterprise corpora… may spend most of their corpus" in the fallback bucket. A stance whose own headline differentiator doesn't activate for most realistic deployments cannot be the skeleton, however good its secondary mechanisms are.
- **memory-os** (4th, 130.5) has the cleanest answer to CI-06's named-open erasure problem (crypto-shred at the key layer) but its two headline mechanisms both carry real production risk the enterprise-operator lens measured most precisely: namespace partitioning multiplies combinatorially under real entitlement lattices, and crypto-shred-by-default makes the KMS a catastrophic single point of failure. Both survive as bounded grafts (§3.3), not as unconditional defaults.
- **learned-policy** (5th, 128.0) scores lowest and, tellingly, research-reviewer's own fatal-flaw line for it is the most useful data point in the whole panel for a synthesis task: it is *"meta-infrastructure for tuning eight IR-pipeline decision points… not a corpus/security/write-path substrate,"* and is *"less a competing next-gen framework than a valuable orchestration layer that could sit on top of any of the other four proposals' substrates."* That is exactly its role in Datum — not a skeleton candidate, but the single most load-bearing graft in the entire document (§3.4), because it is the only stance that supplies the debiasing and promotion-gate discipline systems-db's own Optimizer needs to stop being vaporware.

## 4. Contradictions resolved

Named once here with the reasoning behind each choice; the resulting mechanism is stated as design content in `FRAMEWORK.md` (cross-referenced below) rather than repeated in full.

**#1 — Determinism (systems-db, evidence-first) vs. adaptivity (learned-policy).** A plan that must be exactly reproducible and a plan-selection policy that is supposed to keep improving look, on the surface, like they cannot both be true. Three options were on the table, not two. *(a) Freeze plan-selection forever and never adapt* — the pure-determinism option every systems-db-lineage design defaults to; discarded because it is CI-14's own disease restated as a virtue (a frozen default is exactly what the taxonomy documents rotting silently for years). *(b) Adapt freely and accept that historical plans become unreproducible once the champion policy changes* — the pure-adaptivity option; discarded because it breaks CI-25's reproducibility requirement outright and makes CI-11's trace worthless for audit the moment any promotion happens, which is a strictly worse position than either extreme. *(c) Pin the policy version in the plan manifest* — the naive middle fix; insufficient on its own, because a stochastic policy at a fixed version can still have picked differently on the day, so version-pinning alone loses exactly the information a real audit needs. The resolution actually adopted (G14, replay-by-record) is a refinement of (c): it records the *realized action and its logged propensity*, not just the policy version, and makes replay an explicit choice between "reconstruct what happened" (default) and "re-run under today's champion" (a separate, named call) rather than silently conflating the two. `FRAMEWORK.md` §Design axioms, axiom 6 and stance-conflict #1; §Core abstractions #5.

**#2 — Minimalism (agent-native) vs. completeness (systems-db's own CAL query language).** Three options were considered. *(a) Drop CAL entirely* — discarded because the Plan Compiler still needs *some* structured intermediate representation to reason over cost, filter algebra, and budget; deleting CAL as a named artifact would not remove that need, it would just mean re-inventing an equivalent internal IR informally, without the discipline of a named, versioned surface — worse, not better. *(b) Require CAL as the mandatory agent-facing surface* — systems-db's own original proposal; discarded because multiple lenses named a new DSL as a real, unminimized adoption tax, and no other stance in the panel asks any consumer to learn a query language at all. *(c, adopted)* keep CAL as internal/expert-only plumbing behind a small, fixed, grep-shaped verb surface that is the *only* thing any model is ever required to learn — getting (a)'s internal expressiveness without (b)'s adoption cost. This is a partial, explicit rejection of part of the winning skeleton's own proposal, made deliberately rather than smuggled in as an implementation detail — see Rejection R5 below. `FRAMEWORK.md` §Anti-scope; §Core abstractions #5, #7.

**#3 — Partition-based ACL (memory-os) vs. predicate-based ACL behind a conformance suite (systems-db, agent-native, learned-policy).** Three of five stances default to a typed predicate; one defaults to physical partitioning; the judge panel flagged a real weakness in each: predicates have a four-year documented history of failing open even with conformance testing (LangChain4j `#2513`, Spring AI `#3577`); partitioning explodes combinatorially under real overlapping-group entitlement lattices. Neither a pure predicate default nor a pure partition default survives the panel's own criticism of its rejected alternative. The resolution partitions the coarse, low-cardinality dimension (tenant, department) and falls back to a conformance-tested predicate for the fine-grained residue (role, trial phase, IRB tier), with low-privilege recall measured continuously on *both* paths as the shared backstop that catches whichever one is silently starving a caller — rather than trusting either mechanism's safety claim unverified. `FRAMEWORK.md` §Subsystems, Security & governance.

**#4 — Registry-bound authority hierarchy (evidence-first) vs. general applicability.** The naive fix — drop authority tiering entirely — would discard a genuinely good mechanism for the minority of deployments (medicine, law, curated internal doc hierarchies) where it does apply. The resolution keeps the field but strips it of axiom status: `authority_tier` defaults to `UNVERIFIED` everywhere no registry exists, and the domain-general mechanism — corroboration count, freshness, `trust_class` — is the one the design's safety claims are actually built on, so the majority of deployments (Slack, wikis, tickets) are not running a degraded version of the design's central bet; they are running its normal mode. `FRAMEWORK.md` §Subsystems, Evidence & trust; stance-conflict #4.

**#5 — "No memory subsystem, full stop" (agent-native's anti-scope) vs. "the write path is the whole point" (memory-os's central thesis).** Both stances are right about different things, which is why this is a genuine synthesis rather than a pick-one, but a third option — agent-native's own explicitly stated one — was on the table and deserves a named reason for being set aside rather than silently dropped. Agent-native's anti-scope proposes: *"a memory product can be built as a Layer-4 consumer that writes into Layer 0 through the same `ingest()` contract everything else uses"* — memory as an external consumer of a generic write API, with no memory-specific primitives inside the kernel at all. This was discarded, specifically, because it leaves CI-06's idempotency and atomic-supersession requirements *unowned at the layer where CI-06 actually bites*: a generic `ingest()` contract guarantees nothing about concurrent-write safety or retry-idempotency unless the caller happens to implement it correctly, and Mem0's own production failures (`#5245` silent drop, `#4892` concurrent corruption) are precisely a case of an external system *not* implementing that discipline by default. Pulling the primitives (`WriteOp`, idempotency keys, atomic supersession) into the kernel means every write — a `DocumentPolicy` ingest or a hypothetical external memory product plugging into the identical path — gets these guarantees by construction, not by the discipline of whoever built the product. agent-native is still right that the *product* decisions (what to extract, when to consolidate, whether an agent gets write tools at all) don't belong in the kernel, and those stay pluggable and off by default exactly as agent-native's anti-scope demands; the correction is narrower than a full reversal — it is "primitives are kernel, policy is not," not "memory is kernel." `FRAMEWORK.md` §Subsystems, Memory & mutation; §Anti-scope.

## 5. Rejections, stated with reasoning (not merely "accepted with bounds")

**R1 — evidence-first's `Executor` owning the agent loop. Rejected outright.** research-reviewer named the specific contradiction: the design's own axiom 7 invokes "trained policies own the loop, not hand-wired pipelines," and its architecture then builds a loop-owning `Executor` anyway. Every other stance in the panel — including systems-db, the winning skeleton — agrees the loop is out of scope. Datum keeps this boundary with no exception.

**R2 — evidence-first's `authority_tier` as a central, load-bearing axiom. Rejected as an axiom.** All three lenses independently converged on the same criticism using almost the same words: the mechanism is generalized from one medical-vertical ablation (Dukes et al., OR 3.74 vs. P=.22, one clinical corpus) into a universal design commitment, and most enterprise corpora will sit entirely in the fallback bucket. A design whose headline differentiator doesn't activate for the median deployment cannot make that differentiator load-bearing. The field survives, demoted to optional and registry-bound (§4, #4).

**R3 — memory-os's namespace partitioning as the *only* default ACL mechanism, unbounded. Rejected in that form.** Both lenses that graft-cited it did so with an explicit condition attached (a fan-out ceiling, a measured metric) rather than endorsing it unconditionally — the endorsement itself carries the rejection of the unbounded version. Accepted only as the coarse-dimension default, paired with a conformance-tested predicate for the residue (§4, #3).

**R4 — learned-policy's literal, fixed eight-decision-point list. Rejected as a literal enumeration; the discipline is retained.** Loom's eight points were drawn against the project brief's own decision-point framing, not against systems-db's plan-compiler architecture. Several of Loom's points (granularity, parse-route) are, in Datum's architecture, properties of *which View gets built* — a Derivation Engine decision made at ingest time — not a per-query decision point in the Plan Compiler's slot registry. Forcing the literal list onto a different architecture would be a category mismatch; the tiering discipline (closed, numbered, declared default, declared feedback floor, stated amendment cost) is what actually generalizes, and is redrawn against Datum's own five plan-compiler slots (`FRAMEWORK.md` §Subsystems, Learning & adaptation).

**R5 — systems-db's own CAL query language as a *mandatory* agent-facing surface. Rejected — a partial self-rejection of the winning skeleton.** Multiple lenses named a new DSL as a real, unminimized adoption tax. This is the most intellectually honest rejection in this ledger precisely because it overrides part of the stance that won the skeleton contest rather than only critiquing the four that lost. CAL survives as optional expert/internal plumbing; no consumer is ever required to learn it (§4, #2).

**R6 — systems-db's cost-based Optimizer as the design's headline mechanism. Rejected as the thesis; retained as an implementation slot.** The single most consequential correction in this document, forced by unanimous agreement across all three lenses that the Optimizer is aspirational, not built. Demoting it to a policy slot filled at v1 with a declared, dated rule table — improvable later via G11's promotion-gate machinery — is what lets the rest of the skeleton's genuinely load-bearing engineering (the Plan IR, EXPLAIN/replay, conformance-gated operators, WAL/lineage) ship without waiting on research that may take years. See §2.2.

## 6. Name

**Datum**, chosen over "Strata" (used twice already), "Legend," "Warrant," and "Loom." The metaphor is geodetic: a datum is the fixed reference frame a coordinate is meaningless without — and a vector is meaningless without knowing which embedder produced it, against which corpus snapshot, exactly the reason `View` lineage, dual-read cutover, and drift reports exist. It also carries the Latin root of "data" — "that which is given" — a fit for a design whose write path is a typed algebra of assertions (`WriteOp.assert_`) and whose central security mechanism is an evidentiary envelope. Alternates considered and rejected only for being less apt, not for any deficiency: **Keel** (a ship's structural spine — stable while everything above it, sails and course, changes constantly; the more distinctive brand candidate), **Meridian** (a reference line for navigation, echoing the "coordinate space" language several source proposals used for cross-channel comparability), **Chassis** (a vehicle's stable base frame that everything bolts onto and swaps model-year to model-year).
